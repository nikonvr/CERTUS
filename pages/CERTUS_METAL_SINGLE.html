CERTUS-METAL SINGLE

Single-layer optical constant extraction on a transparent substrate

Scientific report revision 2026.04.17

Abstract: CERTUS-METAL SINGLE estimates the complex refractive index of a single metallic or absorbing layer deposited on a transparent substrate (fused silica or BK7). The method combines reflectance, transmission, and backside reflectance measurements to constrain the inverse problem and extract the layer thickness ($d_M$) together with spline-based optical constants ($n$, $k$).

1. Measurement Context

A single absorbing layer on a transparent substrate provides three complementary observables. The transparent substrate enables transmission measurements in addition to front reflectance and backside reflectance.

  • Transmission ($T$): Constrains absorption through the substrate.
  • Backside reflectance ($R_{back}$): Probes the film-substrate interface.
  • Front reflectance ($R$): Probes the air-film interface.

The joint use of $R$, $T$, and $R_{back}$ reduces non-uniqueness relative to reflectance-only inversion.

2. Theoretical Model

2.1 Layer Structure

The physical model is:

Air | Metal ($d_M$, Spline $n,k$) | Transparent Substrate (Incoherent)

Incoherent Substrate Modeling: The substrate (typically ~0.5–1 mm thick) is much thicker than the coherence length of light. Interference fringes inside the substrate are therefore averaged out. The model analytically sums intensities rather than amplitudes for the substrate traversing paths.
  • Substrate: Selectable Fused Silica or BK7 (Sellmeier dispersion).
  • Metal Film: Modeled with free-form cubic splines for $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$.

2.2 Transfer Matrix Method (Macleod Convention)

The characteristic matrix for the single metal layer uses the Macleod convention ($\hat{n} = n - ik$, factor +1j):

$$ \mathbf{M}_{metal} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi & \frac{+i\sin\phi}{\hat{n}} \\ +i\hat{n}\sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\hat{n}\, d_M $$

2.3 Incoherent Substrate Combination

The front-surface TMM gives coherent $R_{front}$, $T_{front}$. The substrate back-surface reflects with Fresnel intensity coefficient:

$$ R_{ext} = \left(\frac{n_{sub} - 1}{n_{sub} + 1}\right)^2, \qquad T_{ext} = 1 - R_{ext} $$

The total observables are computed by the exact incoherent sum (geometric series):

$$ T_{total} = \frac{T_{front} \cdot T_{ext}}{1 - R_{back} \cdot R_{ext}}, \qquad R_{total} = R_{front} + \frac{T_{front}^2 \cdot R_{ext}}{1 - R_{back} \cdot R_{ext}} $$

$R_{back}$ is computed from a reverse matrix (Sub→Air), ensuring correct treatment even for absorbing films where $R_{front} \neq R_{back}$.

2.4 Spline-Based Material Model

The optical constants are reconstructed as free-form curves:

$$ \hat{n}(\lambda) = S_n(\lambda) - i S_k(\lambda) \quad \text{where} \quad S_{n,k}(\lambda) = a_j + b_j(\lambda - \lambda_j) + c_j(\lambda - \lambda_j)^2 + d_j(\lambda - \lambda_j)^3 $$
  • Knots: Adaptive control points $(n_i, k_i)$ at wavelengths $\lambda_i$.
  • Natural Cubic Splines: $C^2$-continuous interpolation between knots.
  • Physics Constraints: $n > 0$, $k \ge 0$.
  • Knot Distance Constraint: Minimum spacing $\Delta\lambda_{min}$ prevents over-fitting.

3.1 Optimization Vector

The optimization vector contains the following parameters:

  • $d_M$: metal thickness.
  • $\{n_i, k_i\}$: optical constants at the knot positions.
  • $\{\lambda_i\}_{i=2}^{N-1}$: internal knot wavelengths, adjusted to the measured spectrum.

3.2 Multi-Channel Cost Function

The total error is computed as the average over all available data channels:

$$ \text{MSE}_{total} = \frac{1}{N_{ch}} \sum_{c \in \{R,T,R_{back}\}} \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \left(S_c^{calc}(\lambda_j) - S_c^{meas}(\lambda_j)\right)^2 $$

The combined channels constrain the film response at both interfaces and in the bulk absorption region. Missing channels are excluded automatically.

3.3 Differential Evolution

The global search uses Differential Evolution (DE), a population-based stochastic optimizer suitable for high-dimensional and non-convex problems.

  1. Initialize: generate a population of parameter vectors within the bounds.
  2. Mutate: build trial vectors using weighted differences: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{x}_{r1} + F(\mathbf{x}_{r2} - \mathbf{x}_{r3})$
  3. Crossover: combine trial and target vectors with probability CR.
  4. Select: retain the vector with the lower mean squared error.

Typical parameters are: population size 50, $F \in [0.5, 1.0]$, CR = 0.7, and a maximum of 1500 iterations.

4. Beam Analysis: Uncertainty Quantification

4.1 Uniqueness Assessment

Although the combined $R$, $T$, and $R_{back}$ measurements strongly constrain the solution, experimental noise can still produce ambiguity between thickness and refractive index. The beam-analysis post-processing stage maps this uncertainty explicitly.

4.2 Scan Methodology

  1. Reference: start from the optimal thickness $d_{opt}$ returned by the main solver.
  2. Constraint Scan: vary the thickness in both directions using 1 nm steps. At each step, fix $d$ and re-optimize the spline optical constants.
  3. Valley Mapping: plot the minimum MSE as a function of thickness.

4.3 Interpretation

  • V-shape: a sharp V indicates a unique solution.
  • Flat bottom: this indicates parameter correlation, mainly between $d$ and $n$.
  • Beam envelope: the software exports the set of $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$ curves whose MSE remains within the selected tolerance from the global minimum. This defines the admissible process window.

4.4 Uncertainty Formula

The spectral uncertainty envelope defines the confidence intervals used to describe the process window:

$$ \Delta n(\lambda) = \max_{d \in D_\tau} n_{sol}(\lambda, d) - \min_{d \in D_\tau} n_{sol}(\lambda, d) $$ $$ \Delta k(\lambda) = \max_{d \in D_\tau} k_{sol}(\lambda, d) - \min_{d \in D_\tau} k_{sol}(\lambda, d) $$ $$ \text{with } D_\tau = \{ d \mid \text{MSE}(d) \le \text{MSE}_{opt} \cdot (1 + \tau) \} $$

5. Usage Guide

5.1 Input Data Format

CSV or Excel file with up to four auto-detected columns:

Column Content Unit
1 Wavelength ($\lambda$) nm
2 Reflectance ($R$) 0–1 or percent
3 (optional) Transmission ($T$) 0–1 or percent
4 (optional) Backside reflectance ($R_{back}$) 0–1 or percent

5.2 Configuration Guidelines

  • Knots: start with two or three knots for simple metals. Increase the knot count only if the fit remains inadequate.
  • Thickness range: constrain $d_M$ to approximately ±20% of the target deposition.
  • Substrate: the substrate selection (fused silica or BK7) affects the $R_{back}$ and $T$ baselines.

6. Output and Reporting

  • Excel report: fit summary, calculated spectra, and derived $n$ and $k$ tables.
  • Visualization tabs:
    • Reflectance: target versus calculated $R$.
    • n and k: reconstructed optical constants with knot locations.
    • Convergence: log-scale RMSE trace.
    • Beam: uncertainty envelope from the thickness scan.
