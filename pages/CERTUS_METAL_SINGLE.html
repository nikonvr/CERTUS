CERTUS-METAL SINGLE

Single-layer optical constant extraction on a transparent substrate

Implementation review: 2026-10-03

Abstract: CERTUS-METAL SINGLE estimates the complex refractive index of a single metallic or absorbing layer deposited on a transparent substrate. The method combines reflectance, transmission, and backside reflectance measurements to constrain the inverse problem and extract the layer thickness ($d_M$) together with spline-based optical constants ($n$, $k$).

1. Measurement Context

A single absorbing layer on a transparent substrate provides three complementary observables. The transparent substrate enables transmission measurements in addition to front reflectance and backside reflectance.

  • Transmission ($T$): Constrains absorption through the substrate.
  • Backside reflectance ($R_{back}$): Probes the film-substrate interface.
  • Front reflectance ($R$): Probes the air-film interface.

The joint use of $R$, $T$, and $R_{back}$ reduces non-uniqueness relative to reflectance-only inversion.

2. Theoretical Model

2.1 Layer Structure

The physical model is:

Air | Metal ($d_M$, Spline $n,k$) | Transparent Substrate (Incoherent)

Incoherent Substrate Modeling: The substrate (typically ~0.5–1 mm thick) is much thicker than the coherence length of light. Interference fringes inside the substrate are therefore averaged out. The model analytically sums intensities rather than amplitudes for the substrate traversing paths.
  • Substrate: uses the selected Sellmeier catalogue model for a transparent material. Check the selected material: labels without a Sellmeier ID fall back to N-BK7 in _resolve_single_substrate_id.
  • Metal Film: Modeled with free-form cubic splines for $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$.

2.2 Transfer Matrix Method (Macleod Convention)

The characteristic matrix for the single metal layer uses the Macleod convention ($\hat{n} = n - ik$, factor +1j):

$$ \mathbf{M}_{metal} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi & \frac{+i\sin\phi}{\hat{n}} \\ +i\hat{n}\sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\hat{n}\, d_M $$

2.3 Incoherent Substrate Combination

The front-surface TMM gives coherent $R_{front}$, $T_{front}$. The substrate back-surface reflects with Fresnel intensity coefficient:

$$ R_{ext} = \left(\frac{n_{sub} - 1}{n_{sub} + 1}\right)^2, \qquad T_{ext} = 1 - R_{ext} $$

For a transparent substrate, the implementation combines the two faces through an incoherent intensity sum. Here $R_b$ and $T_b$ are the reflectance and transmittance of the film stack illuminated from inside the substrate; $R_{back,total}$ is the observable measured from the rear:

$$ D=1-R_bR_{ext},\qquad T_{total}=\frac{T_{front}(1-R_{ext})}{D},\qquad R_{total}=R_{front}+\frac{T_{front}T_bR_{ext}}{D} $$ $$ R_{back,total}=R_{ext}+\frac{(1-R_{ext})^2R_b}{D} $$

The reverse stack calculation is necessary for absorbing films, where front and rear reflectance differ. The code clamps a near-zero denominator; the equations above show the non-singular case.

2.4 Spline-Based Material Model

The optical constants are reconstructed as free-form curves:

$$ \hat{n}(\lambda) = S_n(\lambda) - i S_k(\lambda) \quad \text{where} \quad S_{n,k}(\lambda) = a_j + b_j(\lambda - \lambda_j) + c_j(\lambda - \lambda_j)^2 + d_j(\lambda - \lambda_j)^3 $$
  • Knots: Adaptive control points $(n_i, k_i)$ at wavelengths $\lambda_i$.
  • Natural Cubic Splines: $C^2$-continuous interpolation between knots.
  • Bounds: the configured $n$ and $k$ knot values default to $[0,10]$; the objective also rejects invalid or non-finite curves.
  • Knot Distance Constraint: Minimum spacing $\Delta\lambda_{min}$ prevents over-fitting.

3.1 Optimization Vector

The optimization vector contains the following parameters:

  • $d_M$: metal thickness.
  • $\{n_i, k_i\}$: optical constants at the knot positions.
  • $\{\lambda_i\}_{i=2}^{N-1}$: internal knot wavelengths, adjusted to the measured spectrum.

3.2 Objective for the Available Channels

_single_RTRback_mse averages the finite MSE terms for $R$, $T$, and $R_{back}$ when both optional channels are supplied. Otherwise it uses $R$ alone. The objective then penalizes rapid changes in the reconstructed spline curves:

$$ J = \frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\operatorname{nanmean}\bigl[(S_c^{calc}-S_c^{meas})^2\bigr] + 10^{-2}\left(\sum_j(\Delta^2 n_j)^2+\sum_j(\Delta^2 k_j)^2\right). $$

$C$ contains only channels with finite residuals when the $T$/$R_{back}$ pair is present. If either optional channel is absent, the current objective uses reflectance alone. The displayed RMSE is the square root of the complete objective.

3.3 PGLOBAL and Knot Continuation

The current global search calls the native PGlobalOptimizer through run_pglobal_optimization. Without a supplied starting vector, it fits knot counts $K=2,\ldots,K_{requested}$ in sequence. An initial mesh seeds the search, each stage explores bounded candidates, and the best vector seeds the next stage. The final stage uses bounded L-BFGS-B restarts without a supplied analytic Jacobian; the separate beam refit supplies an analytic gradient. Iteration and evaluation budgets are configurable; the result is a best-found fit, not a guarantee of the global optimum.

4. Beam Analysis: Local Fit Diagnostic

4.1 Uniqueness Assessment

Although the combined $R$, $T$, and $R_{back}$ measurements strongly constrain the solution, experimental noise can still produce ambiguity between thickness and refractive index. The optional beam-analysis stage samples how fitted optical constants change when thickness is fixed at nearby values.

4.2 Scan Methodology

  1. Reference: start from the optimal thickness $d_{opt}$ returned by the main solver.
  2. Constraint scan: vary thickness in both directions by 0.5 nm, up to 20 nm from the optimum (and no lower than 1 nm). Hold the internal knot wavelengths fixed and re-optimize the $n$ and $k$ knot values against the reflectance channel.
  3. Valley Mapping: plot the minimum MSE as a function of thickness.
flowchart TD A["Best fitted metal thickness"] --> B["Sample both sides in 0.5 nm steps"] B --> C["Fix thickness and internal knot wavelengths"] C --> D["Refit metal n and k knot values against reflectance"] D --> E{"Objective within heuristic threshold?"} E -->|Yes| F["Retain fitted curves"] E -->|No| G["Discard this scan point"] F --> H["Plot MSE by thickness and retained n/k envelopes"] G --> H

4.3 Interpretation

  • Narrow valley: the local fit deteriorates quickly as thickness moves from this optimum; other basins are not ruled out.
  • Flat valley: nearby thicknesses can be compensated by changes in fitted optical constants.
  • Beam envelope: the retained $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$ curves show sensitivity to the scanned thickness range and the chosen fit threshold.

4.4 Uncertainty Formula

The curve envelope is a local fit diagnostic. It is not a statistically calibrated confidence interval or a fabrication process window:

$$ \Delta n(\lambda) = \max_{d \in D} n_{sol}(\lambda, d) - \min_{d \in D} n_{sol}(\lambda, d) $$ $$ \Delta k(\lambda) = \max_{d \in D} k_{sol}(\lambda, d) - \min_{d \in D} k_{sol}(\lambda, d) $$ $$ \text{with } D = \{ d \text{ sampled at 0.5 nm intervals} \mid J(d) \le \max(1.5J_{opt}+10^{-5},\,5\times10^{-5}) \}. $$

5. Usage Guide

5.1 Input Data Format

CSV or Excel file with up to four auto-detected columns:

Column Content Unit
1 Wavelength ($\lambda$) nm
2 Reflectance ($R$) 0–1 or percent
3 (optional) Transmission ($T$) 0–1 or percent
4 (optional) Backside reflectance ($R_{back}$) 0–1 or percent

5.2 Configuration Guidelines

  • Knots: start with two or three knots for simple metals. Increase the knot count only if the fit remains inadequate.
  • Thickness range: constrain $d_M$ to approximately ±20% of the target deposition.
  • Substrate: the resolved transparent-substrate model affects the $R_{back}$ and $T$ baselines.

6. Output and Reporting

  • Excel report: fit summary, calculated spectra, and derived $n$ and $k$ tables.
  • Visualization tabs:
    • Reflectance: target versus calculated $R$.
    • n and k: reconstructed optical constants with knot locations.
    • Convergence: log-scale RMSE trace.
    • Beam: uncertainty envelope from the thickness scan.

8. Pro 2026 Ergonomics & UI Upgrades

The fit runs in a worker and reports progress while the GUI remains responsive. Numerical kernels use NumPy and Numba on the paths called by the model.

8.1 Controls

Use the window's Load, Run, Stop, Save and Beam controls. Keyboard bindings should be read from the running window's shortcuts help, since a command shown in a palette is not necessarily bound to a key.

The long-running numerical tasks are executed in isolated worker threads. The interface uses wait cursors, tooltips, and progress feedback to keep the event loop responsive.
