REVISION 2026-08-04 — this principle was half-true, it is now fully true

The propagation described above was indeed implemented, but its effect was completely neutralized at the cut-off moment. The trigger level was recalculated on the actual stack: with the inversion parabola interpolating exactly this same point, the resolution yielded Δd = noise / P′ and nothing else. At zero noise, a layer would come out nominal regardless of the errors accumulated before it. No compensation could appear or be measured.

Fix 1 — fixed target. The trigger level is now calculated on the NOMINAL stack, just like in the clean room where it is fixed before deposition. The inversion remains performed on the actual stack. The difference Tnom(d) − Treal(d) reappears in the numerator: this is the compensation term.

Fix 2 — POEM (Percent of Optical Extrema Monitoring), according to Arsac, 2025 thesis, eq. 2.2, and Zideluns et al., Opt. Express 29, 33398 (2021):

TPOEM = (Ttrigger − Tprev TP) / (Tlast TP − Tprev TP)

The cut-off point is no longer a transmission value but a fraction of the amplitude between the last two turning points, pre-calculated on the nominal stack and then transferred to the actually observed extrema. Any affine distortion of the signal — photometric gain or offset, refractive index error, upstream thickness error — is absorbed by construction. Compensation is achieved by change of variable, not by a coefficient. Sweeping at 64 points over 3× the nominal thickness, to have the virtual next turning points when the layer contains fewer than two; fallback to absolute target under 4% amplitude.

Two coefficients removed. non_monotonic_factor (halved the error as soon as an extremum was crossed) and wavelength_change_penalty (increased the noise of the first layer of a block) were the reduced forms of a mechanism the model could not produce. It is now structural; applying them in addition would double-count the same effect.

HARD failure — the deposition that never finishes

If the theoretical cut-off falls just BEFORE a turning point, an upstream error can make the signal turn before having reached the targeted value. The controller then waits for a level that will never come and the deposition crashes. This is not a loss of precision, it is a crash — a discrete event, structurally invisible to an RMSE criterion.

Conversely, stopping AFTER a turning point is safe: the extremum is already counted, the signal moves away from it monotonically, the level is inevitably reached. This is the physical justification for the asymmetry of check_extrema_proximity — forbidden zone 3× wider before a turning point than after.

The kernel now detects two hard failures, penalty 1e6: the targeted level is not bracketed by the actual signal between the start of the layer and the next extremum; and the counted number of extrema diverges between nominal and actual — in which case the controller anchors POEM on the wrong turning points. This second case requires detecting the extrema on the ACTUAL signal, the one the controller measures, and not on the nominal one: detecting on the nominal one would grant the simulator knowledge that the controller does not have.

1. Physical Model (Transfer Matrix Method)

The engine uses the Macleod Transfer Matrix Method (TMM), JIT-compiled through Numba with vectorized and threaded execution.

1.1 Conventions

  • Complex refractive index: $\hat{n} = n - ik$ with $k > 0$ for absorption (Macleod standard convention).
  • Matrix factor: $+i$ (I_VAL = +1j) in off-diagonal elements.
  • Accumulation order: Pre-multiply Air→Substrate. The system matrix is built as $M = L_1 \cdot L_2 \cdot \ldots \cdot L_N$, where layer 1 is on the air side and layer $N$ is on the substrate side.
  • Precision: All internal computations use float64 / complex128 to avoid mixed-precision recompilation.
  • Index consistency: All index arrays remain complex128 throughout, including the Phase A extrema checks and the Phase B robustness batch.

1.2 Characteristic Matrix of a Single Layer

For layer $j$ with complex index $\hat{n}_j$ and physical thickness $d_j$ at normal incidence:

$$ \delta_j = \frac{2\pi \hat{n}_j d_j}{\lambda} $$ $$ L_j = \begin{pmatrix} \cos \delta_j & \dfrac{+i}{\hat{n}_j} \sin \delta_j \\[6pt] +i\,\hat{n}_j \sin \delta_j & \cos \delta_j \end{pmatrix} $$

1.3 System Matrix and Transmittance

The total system matrix for a stack of $N$ layers is built by pre-multiplication from Air to substrate:

$$ M = \prod_{j=1}^{N} L_j = L_1 \cdot L_2 \cdots L_N $$ $$ T = \frac{4\,\text{Re}(\hat{n}_{sub})}{|M_{00} + \hat{n}_{sub}\,M_{01} + M_{10} + \hat{n}_{sub}\,M_{11}|^2} $$

where $n_{inc} = 1$ (air).

1.4 Incoherent Backside Correction

The substrate back face reflects incoherently. CERTUS uses a dual-matrix approach in calculate_detailed_growth:

$$ T_{total} = \frac{T_{fwd} \cdot T_{ext}}{1 - R_{rev} \cdot R_{ext}} $$
  • $T_{fwd}$: Forward transmittance from the F matrix (Air→Sub, pre-multiply).
  • $R_{rev}$: Reverse reflectance from the R matrix (Sub→Air, post-multiply) — a separate matrix accumulation, required for absorbing multilayers where $R_{fwd} \neq R_{rev}$.
  • $R_{ext} = \left(\frac{n_s - 1}{n_s + 1}\right)^2$: Fresnel reflectance at the substrate/air interface.
  • $T_{ext} = 1 - R_{ext}$.
  • Validity check: validate_backside_real_indices warns once per run if $|k_{layer}|$ or $|k_{sub}|$ exceed thresholds (constants K_MAX_LAYER_BACKSIDE, K_MAX_SUBSTRATE_BACKSIDE), since the incoherent approximation degrades for absorbing materials.

1.5 Growth Dynamics Kernel

compute_dynamics_kernel evaluates $T(d)$ continuously as a layer grows from $d=0$ to $d=d_{nom}$ in 1 nm steps. The dynamics metric is the peak-to-peak range:

$$ \Delta T(\lambda) = \max_{d \in [0,\,d_{nom}]} T(d) - \min_{d \in [0,\,d_{nom}]} T(d) \quad \text{(T}_{max} - T_{min}\text{)} $$

A large $\Delta T$ means the monitoring signal changes significantly during deposition at this wavelength, providing a strong trigger signal for the cut-off controller. Candidates for which $T_{min}$ (minimum T over full layer growth) falls below the minimum transmission floor (default 10%, parameter min_transmission_floor) are strictly excluded. This strict floor is enforced both during Phase A candidate selection and in a post-check on the final best strategy.

2.2 Smoothing changes the DIRECTION of the detection threshold

Section 2.5 derives the hysteresis threshold from an amplitude bound on the raw readings: the draw being bounded at $\pm A$, the maximum difference that noise alone can display is $2A$, so a threshold $\ge 2A$ forbids any false inversion. This reasoning is correct — but it applies to a machine that would read raw, and the OMS does not read raw.

📖 This page already anticipated it: Zideluns p. 112 notes that the amplitude threshold can be lowered "by increasing the signal processing time", which §2.4 commented on with "the machine filters". 👤 Confirmed in 2026: the average covers about 2 seconds.

Smoothing acts on two fronts, and the second dominates:

  • Amplitude — averaging $k$ readings divides the standard deviation by $\sqrt{k}$. At $k = 8$, the jitter drops from 0.10 to 0.035 points.
  • Correlation — two neighboring sliding averages share 7 readings out of 8 and therefore cannot deviate sharply. Fabricating a false inversion would require a coherent flipping of the noise over the entire window, much rarer than a reading high followed by a low reading.

Counter-intuitive consequence: the threshold DROPS. Keeping tp_hysteresis_factor expressed in multiples of the raw amplitude $A$, the criterion "3σ of the smoothed signal" is written:

$$ \texttt{tp\_hysteresis\_factor} \;=\; \frac{3\,\sigma_{\text{smoothed}}}{A} \;=\; \frac{3}{3\sqrt{k}} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}} \qquad k = 8 \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathbf{0{,}354} $$

Versus 1.66 previously: 4.7× lower, on a signal 2.8× quieter. This is better on both sides of the problem — fewer false turning points and better detection of faint true extrema, which is the side no one had measured.

2.3 Noise Model — Gaussian (Production Mode)

generate_noise_array generates gaussian noise for all Monte Carlo simulations. This is the production mode in STRAT.

$$ \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\; \sigma^2/9), \quad \epsilon \text{ clipped to } [-\sigma, +\sigma] $$
  • $\sigma$ = trigger_tolerance / 100: The noise scale is derived from the spectrophotometer trigger tolerance parameter.
  • Gaussian: clipped to ±3σ (i.e., $\sigma_{raw} = \frac{1}{3}$) for numerical stability — this matches the 99.7% envelope of realistic sensor noise.
  • Deterministic pipeline: Phase A uses a globally pre-allocated noise matrix global_noise_matrix[runs × layers] generated once from phase_a_seed. Each layer gets a fixed column, ensuring reproducibility regardless of thread ordering.
  • Phase B uses COMMON RANDOM NUMBERS. The noise matrix depends only on (robustness_seed, noise_idx, num_runs, num_layers) and is served from a cache (_get_cached_sobol_noise, certus_strat_robustness.py:482-510) — it does not depend on the strategy. The _strat_idx argument of _test_strategy_robustness_task is underscore-prefixed and deliberately unused (:524).
    This is the right design and it matters: every strategy faces the same noise realisations, so the comparison between two strategies is paired and the noise largely cancels in the difference — which is the only thing a ranking depends on.
    ⚠️ A previous version of this page claimed seed = robustness_seed + strategy_idx × 10⁵ + noise_idx. That was wrong: it would have given each strategy an independent draw and made the ranking far noisier.
    The Sobol seed itself is scrambled — base_seed × 2 654 435 761 + noise_idx × 40 503 (:509) — because the naive base_seed + noise_idx made distinct pairs collide: (seed 42, level 1) and (seed 43, level 0) both gave 43, hence the same noise.
  • The legacy distribution argument is accepted for backward compatibility but intentionally ignored — behavior is always gaussian.

2.8 The affine invariance of POEM — the theorem, and why the code denied it

The argument that justifies POEM fits on one line. If the instrument undergoes a photometric calibration drift — a gain $a$ and a shift $b$ unknown to the controller — the measured signal is $T_{\text{mes}} = a\,T + b$. The two anchors undergo the same transformation, so the level reported by POEM does too:

$$ T_{\text{target}}^{\text{mes}} \;=\; a T_{\text{prev}} + b \;+\; p\left[(a T_{\text{last}} + b) - (a T_{\text{prev}} + b)\right] \;=\; a\left[T_{\text{prev}} + p\,(T_{\text{last}} - T_{\text{prev}})\right] + b \;=\; a\,T_{\text{target}} + b $$

The controller stops when $a\,T(d) + b = a\,T_{\text{target}} + b$, i.e. when $T(d) = T_{\text{target}}$: exactly the desired thickness. The compensation is achieved by a change of variable, not by a hand-tuned coefficient. POEM is thus strictly invariant under this class of errors, and the absolute fallback is not — that is the whole point of the mechanism, and it is the only thing a simulation must verify.

🔴 THE CODE CANCELED ITS OWN DISTORTION IN THREE PLACES, and would thus have led to concluding the opposite of the truth. The final parabolic inversion fitted the undistorted model and then solved it against a distorted target — two sides, two units. The absolute fallback pre-distorted its target to $a\,T_{\text{nom}} + b$, which handed back to the controller the calibration it is by hypothesis supposed to have lost. And the minimum swing test scaled its own threshold by $a$, canceling the gain exactly.

2.9 Index uncertainty — a dispersion corridor, not a scalar

👤 What the physicist describes (2026-08-08). The indices are presupposed, known to within ±0.005 — in absolute index units, and it is a half-width. They are really the same for all even layers, and the same for all odd ones: no variation during deposition. But it is not ±0.005 independently at each λ — it is a dispersion curve that can be shifted, or crossed (different slopes), within a 0.005 corridor over the entire spectral domain. And the curves remain smooth.

The resulting model is a perturbation affine in λ, drawn once per run and per material:

$$ \delta_M(\lambda) = a_M + b_M\,u(\lambda), \qquad u(\lambda) = \frac{2\lambda - (\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})}{\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{\min}} \in [-1,1] $$ $$ n_M^{\text{real}}(\lambda) = n_M^{\text{nom}}(\lambda) + \delta_M(\lambda), \qquad |a_M| + |b_M| \le \delta_{\max} = 0{,}005 $$

$b = 0$ gives the pure shift; $a = 0$ gives the pure crossing, the curve intersecting the nominal one in the middle of the domain. The constraint $|a|+|b| \le \delta_{\max}$ guarantees the corridor everywhere, and the affinity guarantees the smoothness. A quadratic term would be superfluous: the physicist names two modes, and the affine covers them exactly.

🔴 SHIFT AND CROSSING DO NOT COST THE SAME THING, and that is the heart of the matter. The monitoring corrects the optical thickness at a single wavelength, $\lambda_{\text{mon}}$. A shifted curve is shifted identically everywhere: the correction made at $\lambda_{\text{mon}}$ holds roughly for the whole domain, the error is largely compensable. A crossed curve carries an error of opposite sign on either side of the crossing: the correction made at $\lambda_{\text{mon}}$ worsens the error on the other side. It is uncompensable by design.

Prediction to be measured, not a given: for the same corridor, $b$ should be far more destructive than $a$. If confirmed, this favors strategies whose control λs are spread across the domain rather than grouped — exactly the kind of trade-off STRAT exists to find. Draw and report $a$ and $b$ separately, to be able to attribute.

⚠️ The absolute corridor bites harder on the low index. 0.005 on $n_H = 2{,}35$ is 0.21% relative; on $n_L = 1{,}46$ it is 0.34%. Since the optical thickness is $n\,d$, the relative optical thickness error is the relative index error: the L layers undergo a perturbation 1.6× larger than the H layers, for the same corridor. $\delta_{\max}$ is therefore a single absolute constant, especially not a fraction of $n$.

📏 The kernel used to produce exactly zero, because indices were deterministic there. The obvious fix — pre-multiplying $n_H$ and $n_L$ at the call site — was inert: $3{,}3\cdot10^{-10}$ nm of response at $\delta = 0{,}005$ and $1{,}9\cdot10^{-10}$ nm at $\delta = 0{,}05$, i.e. ten times the perturbation for no effect. The reason was structural: the same set of indices drove the real stack and the nominal stack, and shifting them together creates no divergence. Two sets are needed — one for what is deposited, one for what the controller expects.

2.10 Rate mode — control by turn counting, and what it costs

👤 What the physicist describes (2026-08-09). The OMS 5100 offers a rate mode with time-based control, counting the rotations of the substrate holder. The deposition rate is estimated from the previous layers — even ones on one side, odd ones on the other, i.e. per material — by comparing the number of turns observed against the theoretical thicknesses. The rate dispersion is about ±σ = 1 to 2 %, which is what then allows the number of deposition turns to be computed when the operator selects rate mode.

There is no photometric control and no error compensation during a rate layer: the errors accumulated upstream are neither measured nor corrected, they are carried forward open-loop. The switch criterion is the poverty of the optical dynamics ($\text{swing} < \text{SWING\_MIN}$), not an absolute thickness threshold — a 30 nm layer at very low index contrast can be just as poor as an ultra-thin one.

🔑 The control unit is the TURN, not the second. At 240 rpm one turn lasts 250 ms, i.e. 0.125 nm at 0.5 nm/s — exactly the sampling step of the frozen reading model in §2.1. That is not a coincidence: both come from the same rotation. A rate-mode thickness is therefore quantised in whole turns, and that quantisation costs no parameter — it falls out on its own.

🔑 $\sigma_{\text{rate}}$ is not a parameter. It is a DERIVED quantity. An earlier version of this section posed $d_{\text{real}} = d_{\text{nom}}(1+\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma))$ with $\sigma \in [1\,\%, 2\,\%]$. That was dropped on 2026-08-09, for a reason that is worth stating in full: the simulator knows both of the quantities the machine compares, so it can redo the machine's rate calculation instead of replacing it with a draw.

Let $v$ be the true deposition rate and $t_{\text{turn}} = 0.25$ s, so one turn deposits $q = v\,t_{\text{turn}} = 0.125$ nm.

  • Layer $j$ of material $M$, deposited under POEM: real thickness $d^{\text{real}}_j$, so the machine counted $n_j = d^{\text{real}}_j / q$ turns. But it believes it deposited $d^{\text{nom}}_j$ — that is the target it was aiming at, and nothing tells it otherwise.
  • Its rate estimate is therefore $\widehat{v}_M = d^{\text{nom}}_j / (n_j t_{\text{turn}}) = v\cdot d^{\text{nom}}_j / d^{\text{real}}_j$.
  • Layer $i$ in rate mode: the machine commands $n_i = \operatorname{round}\bigl(d^{\text{nom}}_i / (\widehat{v}_M t_{\text{turn}})\bigr)$ turns, hence deposits $d^{\text{real}}_i = n_i q$:
$$ \frac{d^{\text{real}}_i}{d^{\text{nom}}_i} \;=\; \frac{d^{\text{real}}_j}{d^{\text{nom}}_j} $$

The relative error is not drawn — it is COPIED. Which settles several things at once: there is no free parameter left, so nothing invented replaces something measured; the question "correlated bias or per-layer draw?" dissolves, because it is neither, it is an equality; and both quantities are already in the kernel (prev_thicknesses_sim[j] and p_thick_nominal[j], used side by side), so no plumbing is needed.

🟢 And it turns $\sigma_{\text{rate}}$ into a PREDICTION — which is the first opportunity this project has ever had for an external check. If the simulator returns a dispersion of the same order as what the machine shows on the shop floor, that is corroboration from outside the model. If it returns 0.1 %, the noise model is missing something. Either outcome teaches something, and it costs nothing to look.

⚠️ The one assumption this introduces is that $v$ is constant during a run. If the source really drifts (depletion, temperature), a drift term comes back — but it will then have to be measured, not posed.

🔴 What rate really costs: the existing error is FROZEN, and the POEM history is lost. A rate layer reproduces the relative error of the last layer of the same material, where POEM would have corrected it. On top of that, the next layer starts over classically — the block's POEM anchors are no longer usable. Deliberately introducing rate on thin or low-dynamics layers of an otherwise promising strategy adds one degree of freedom per layer to the search, and it belongs inside the existing dynamic programming, not in a phase of its own: its cost is a block property, which is exactly what the DP already arbitrates. The trade-off — error repeated on a thin layer against a broken POEM chain — is the kind of arbitration the statistical study exists to settle.

⚠️ Not implemented. No rate mode exists in the kernel today. This section records the specification, not the code.

🔑 And the bias is a PROFILE along the layer's growth, not one number per layer — that distinction is the whole point. A constant added to every reading is exactly the b of T → a·T + b, which POEM is rigorously invariant under: measured, a constant of 10−2 — twenty times the reading noise — moves the stopping thickness by 1.8×10−11 nm. A per-layer scalar therefore modelled the one shape POEM absorbs for free. The bias varies by up to 62 A inside a single layer near the band edge, and it is that variation POEM cannot absorb.

🔑 THE MECHANISM, stated exactly (👤 2026-08-10): "at no point does the OMS know how to compute spectral responses with a resolution problem — it is always with a perfect resolution. That is why opening the slits too much can be a problem: the expected levels are not the right ones."

So the machine compares two quantities that are not of the same kind: it expects $T_{\text{theory}}(\lambda_{\text{mon}})$, computed monochromatically at perfect resolution, and it reads $\langle T\rangle_B$, averaged over the slit. The difference is not a loss of dynamics. It is a systematic level bias:

$$ \text{bias}(B) \;=\; \langle T\rangle_B - T(\lambda_{\text{mon}}) \;=\; \frac{T''(\lambda_{\text{mon}})\,B^2}{24} $$

🔴 A bias is not noise. It does not average out, it is not diluted by the Monte-Carlo, it pushes every draw the same way. The machine cuts systematically early or systematically late and has no way of noticing, since its only reference is its own theory — which is monochromatic. An earlier version of this section proposed optimising a ratio $\text{swing}_{\text{eff}}(B)/\text{noise}(B)$: that was wrong. A bias of $X$ and a noise of $X$ are not commensurable, because the statistics absorb the second and not the first.

🔴 Why this is critical, and not a second-order comfort setting

The bias scales with the curvature $T''$. Curvature is maximal at a turning point, where $T' = 0$ by definition. And a turning point is exactly what POEM uses as an anchor.

So a wide slit mis-reads the two anchors the whole method rests on — and it mis-reads them inward, since $T''$ points into the curve on either side of an extremum. The measured swing $|T_{\text{last}} - T_{\text{prev}}|$ therefore shrinks, and POEM applies its frozen fraction to an amplitude that is too small.

And it breaks the invariance theorem. §2.8 proves POEM exactly immune to $T \to aT + b$ — an affine map, the same everywhere. The slit bias is $T \to T + T''(\lambda)B^2/24$: it depends on the local curvature, so it differs at each anchor and at the trigger point. It is precisely the kind of distortion POEM cannot absorb, unlike the photometric drift it defeats by more than an order of magnitude.

Finally, near a turning point $dT/dd \to 0$, so a level error converts into a large thickness error — the very regime §14 warns about. Bias, weakest slope, and the anchors of the mechanism all coincide at the same place.

🔴 These four numbers are ESTIMATES, not measurements (👤 2026-08-09, verbatim: estimated "by feeling"). They therefore have the status of a modelling postulate, like the frozen reading model of §2.1 — never cite them as measured. The consequence is binding: a conclusion drawn from them is a physical result only if it survives their uncertainty. Every resolution measurement must be repeated with the factors displaced over a plausible range (÷1.2 instead of ÷1.5, ×3 instead of ×5). If the winning resolution does not change, the conclusion is robust. If it does, the conclusion rests on a guess and that must be said in the same sentence.

🔒 It is a TABLE, never a fitted law. Four settings, four entries. A power law would not even hold: between 5 and 2 nm the behaviour is close to photon-limited $1/\sqrt{B}$ ($\sqrt{2/5} = 0.63$ against the quoted ÷1.5), but below nominal it degrades faster than $1/B$ (×5 at 0.5 nm where $1/B$ would give ×4). Fitting a law over four estimates would build an invented model on invented numbers — and it would let the search propose resolutions the machine does not have.

🔴 The slit is RECTANGULAR (👤 2026-08-09), and the production criterion is a factor $\sqrt{3}$ too strict. A uniform average over $[\lambda_0 - B/2, \lambda_0 + B/2]$ has second-order error $T''B^2/24$, whereas the second difference the code measures over $\pm\,\text{test\_bw}/2$ is $T''\,\text{test\_bw}^2/8$. The usable bandwidth is therefore $\sqrt{24/8} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ times wider than the code allows. Verified numerically against direct boxcar integration: ratio 1.7321 on a pure quadratic, 1.7305 on a 20 nm cosine, 1.7252 on a 10 nm cosine — the drift showing the second-order expansion degrading exactly where features get sharp. The one-line fix, res_limit = test_bw*sqrt(3*T_tolerance/curvature), widens the admissible slits, which makes the ÷1.5 noise bonus reachable for strategies currently denied it for no reason.

The "too fine costs noise" side is wired too, as a four-entry table; a width the machine does not offer raises rather than being interpolated, because four estimates do not make a law and a law would authorise settings that do not exist on the machine.

🔑 The resolution is part of the STRATEGY, not a setting applied to it (👤 2026-08-09). A strategy is therefore no longer (block partition, λ per block) but (block partition, λ per block, resolution), with a single resolution for the whole run.

It cannot live in Phase A, which judges one layer at a time, because the cost of a resolution is a whole-run compromise: the binding layer is unknown until the strategy exists. It does not belong in the DP either, which optimises a sum of per-layer costs, whereas the resolution is a single global choice that does not decompose additively. It belongs in Phase B, where each candidate strategy is screened at the four resolutions — a ×4 on the Monte-Carlo cost, which is the honest price of measuring the quantity that decides.

🔴 Common random numbers, and this is where they are easiest to lose. The resolution factor must multiply the sample, never enter the seed:

And here is the effect that was not expected. The resolution does not merely add a four-way branch: it changes which wavelengths are good. A λ sitting in a spectrally smooth region tolerates the 5 nm slit and collects its ÷1.5 noise bonus for free; a λ near the edge forces 1 nm and pays ×2. The worth of a wavelength now depends on the spectral smoothness of its neighbourhood, not only on its dynamics. That arbitration appears nowhere in the code today, and it is precisely the kind of thing STRAT exists to find.

⚠️ The compromise is not the same at every layer, and that is the knot. The spectral sharpness of the stack grows with the layer count: a resolution that is comfortable at layer 3 may be too coarse at layer 40. Since it is frozen for the run, the optimum is a compromise over the whole deposition.

2.12 The critical layer, and how to measure a risk that reads zero

👤 The question (2026-08-10): "it would be good, for each strategy, to name the most critical layer -- the one most at risk of a crash. And if the statistics read zero crashes, one would have to raise the noise or the index drift by some factor I do not control."

This is rare-event estimation. At 150 draws with zero crashes one knows $p < 2\,\%$ and nothing more; resolving $p = 0.1\,\%$ would take some 3000 draws per strategy. And raising the noise -- the natural reflex -- is the trap this project has paid for three times (§2.7): POEM's compensation is not linear, so a rate measured at twice the noise does not reduce to the rate at nominal.

🟢 Tier 0 — the per-layer information already exists, and is discarded

n_crash_run   = np.count_nonzero(np.any(crashed_cells, axis=1))   # <- collapses the layers

The array is per (run, layer), and np.any along axis 1 reduces it before anyone has looked. crashed_cells.sum(axis=0) is the crash count per layer. A few lines, no extra runs.

⚠️ But "where it fails" is not "what is responsible". The sentinel is written where the deposition stops; a badly deposited layer upstream can sink a layer downstream by propagation -- which is the whole point of the compensation chain. Report both, never conflate them.

🔑 Tier 1 — the property that changes the textbook answer: the draws are BOUNDED

Classical reliability would answer "Hasofer-Lind index, $\beta = \text{margin}/\sigma$, $p \approx \Phi(-\beta)$". That would be wrong here, and wrong in a knowable direction. Almost every perturbation in this model is bounded: the reading noise is drawn inside $\pm A$, the corridor satisfies $|a| + |b| \le \delta_{\max}$, the affine amplitudes are bounded.

$$\text{margin} > \text{largest possible perturbation} \;\Longrightarrow\; p = 0 \quad\text{exactly, not merely small}$$

§2.2 already uses this — "the draw being bounded at $\pm A$, the largest difference noise alone can show is $2A$". A Gaussian $\beta$ would extrapolate a small probability where the truth is zero, and it would do so always on the pessimistic side. So the first test is not probabilistic at all: it is deterministic.

Tier 2 — measure the margin, not the event

For layers that can fail, stop counting crashes and evaluate a limit-state function: how far was this run from crossing? Continuous, always defined, and informative at zero crashes.

🔴 One margin per layer is too coarse. It must be per layer and per cause: the three share neither units, nor physics, nor remedy. That is corollary 2 of §2.7 -- never let two causes hide under one sentinel. The critical layer is the one with the smallest normalised margin, and it is named even when no run has crashed.

Tier 3 — calibrate the extrapolation where counting still works

This is what separates rigour from cleverness, and it costs no extra run: the three noise levels $0.5\times / 1\times / 2\times$ are already computed. At $2\times$ crashes appear, so they can be counted; the margin model predicts a rate there; the two are compared. If they agree the model is validated on this stack and may be trusted at $1\times$, where counting returns zero. If they disagree it may not be — and that is a result too.

One never extrapolates without first validating the extrapolation in the regime where measurement is possible.

And the margin is a better Trap-1 test than the crash rate: it must scale as $1/\sigma$, and being continuous it is far more sensitive than a rate which jumps from 0 to $1/N$ — at $N = 150$, blind to anything below 0.7 %.

⚠️ Not implemented. Every tier above is paid in code, none in compute — and it answers a question no number of draws would, since a zero rate stays zero however many times it is drawn.

2.13 Non-Monotonic Error Model

Beyond pure trigger noise, the simulation optionally adds a systematic offset via compute_probe_offset_nm_from_ratio. The effective measured thickness incorporates:

2.14 Cumulative Error Propagation

The key innovation of CERTUS is that thickness errors are physically propagated through the TMM. If layer $i$ was deposited with an error $\delta d_i$:

Error Propagation Mechanism:
$$ \delta d_i^{(r)} = d_{nom,i} \cdot \epsilon^{(r)} + \delta_{probe} $$
1. Layer $i$ is deposited with actual thickness $d_i + \delta d_i^{(r)}$ in run $r$.
2. The TMM matrix is updated with this real thickness.
3. The monitoring signal for layer $i+1$ is computed from this perturbed matrix.
4. The cut-off for layer $i+1$ is triggered based on the perturbed signal — which may itself introduce an additional error $\delta d_{i+1}$.
5. This cascade continues for all $N$ layers.

This is implemented in simulate_stack_robustness_batch which processes $N_{runs}$ independent realisations in parallel using Numba prange.

2.15 Reality-Feedback Loop in Phase A

3.4 Step A.3 — Monte Carlo Validation

For each surviving candidate $\lambda_k$:

Vectorized Monte Carlo (Numba validate_wavelengths_batch): 1. Use column $i$ from the pre-allocated global_noise_matrix (gaussian, seeded by phase_a_seed).
2. For each run $r$, simulate the full growth of layer $i$ at wavelength $\lambda_k$ using cumulative history from runs_history_matrix. 3. Compute thickness error: $\Delta d_r = |d_{simulated}^{(r)} - d_{nominal}|$, over the runs that terminate.
4. Return four columns per candidate, not one.

validate_wavelengths_batch returns a $(n_{cands}, 4)$ array: $Q_{0.95}(\Delta d_r)$, its standard deviation, the crash rate, and the compensation gain. The P95 and the standard deviation are computed only on runs that terminate: leaving the $10^6$ sentinel in would mix nanometres with a failure counter.

Cost — two terms, both in nanometres, no fitting constant: $$ \text{Cost}(\lambda_k) = Q_{0.95}\left(\Delta d_r\right) \;+\; w \cdot g(\lambda_k) \cdot \varepsilon_{\text{prev}} $$ $g(\lambda_k)$ is the compensation gain — how much of an upstream error the layer carries forward ($g < 1$ self-compensates, $g > 1$ amplifies). It costs two noise-free evaluations, no Monte Carlo. $\varepsilon_{\text{prev}}$ (phase_a_prev_error_nm) is the P95 measured on the Monte Carlo states actually propagated — measured, not postulated. $w$ is phase_a_compensation_weight, default $1$.

Below 0.05 nm — less than one atom — $\varepsilon_{\text{prev}}$ is floored to zero and the second term vanishes.

4. Phase B: Block Grouping & Robustness Screening

4.1 Dynamic Programming Segmentation

Minimizing monochromator movements is crucial for process speed and reproducibility. CERTUS groups layers into monochromatic blocks (contiguous layers monitored at the same wavelength) using Sequential Dynamic Programming via _dp_kernel / _compute_valid_blocks_kernel (Numba kernels imported from _certus_physics_impl).

$$ V_k(i) = \min_{0 \le j < i} \left\{ V_{k-1}(j) + \min_{\lambda \in \Lambda} \sum_{m=j+1}^{i} \text{Cost}(m, \lambda) \right\} $$

Bellman state equation:

  • $V_k(i)$: cumulative minimum cost (corresponding to DP[k][i]) for covering layers $1 \dots i$ with exactly $k$ blocks.
  • $j$: optimal cut point (end of block $k-1$).
  • $\text{Cost}(m, \lambda)$: cost of layer $m$ monitored at $\lambda$, as defined in §3.4 — $Q_{0.95}(\Delta d) + w \cdot g \cdot \varepsilon_{\text{prev}}$, not the P95 alone. Candidates eliminated on crash rate never reach the DP.
  • The inner minimization loop corresponds to $\text{BlockCost}(j, i)$.

What this DP can see — and what it cannot

1. The cost of a block is the RAW SUM of its layers' costs (certus_strat_dp.py:61), and the DP sums the blocks (:118). The minimized objective is therefore exactly $\sum_{\ell} \text{Cost}(\ell, \lambda_{\text{block}(\ell)})$.
Structural consequence: merging two adjacent blocks at the same λ does not change the cost. The DP is blind to the segmentation — only the constraint $k = n_{\text{blocks}}$ sets it. Yet the physical value of a block is precisely that the signal remains continuous at a constant λ and that self-compensation operates. This value does not appear in the objective. This is what the symmetry bonuses and ELITE passes try to make up for afterwards.

2. n_blocks is a cut count, not a count of monochromatic blocks. Two adjacent blocks at the same λ are counted as two whereas in the chamber it is only one — the signal is not interrupted. The displayed number therefore does not mean what it seems to mean.

3. Only the top 10 λs per block enter the DP (certus_strat_dp.py:117, min(10, bl_count), hardcoded). Measured on the example (scripts/probe_block_wls.py): the cap bites on 38 out of 40 blocks, throwing away a median of 8 candidates out of 18 available. But what it throws away is the tail — the 10th retained one already costs ×2.2 the first one in median — and the 10 retained ones spread over 95 nm in median, covering 4 distinct regions whereas cost(λ) has only 2 to 5 local minima. The structure is thus already captured. The true pruner is not this cap, it's Phase A, which reduces ~51 scanned λs to 1–27 survivors per layer.

4. A block is only valid if ALL its layers have this λ among their Phase A survivors (:64-66). An elimination on a single layer kills the entire block. The enumeration starts from the most constrained layer (:36-45) — a constraint propagation heuristic, and it's a good choice.

5. The DP keeps top_k×2 paths per state (:93). It is indeed a search for the k best paths, not a simple beam search: Bellman optimality is preserved for the given objective. The problem is not the algorithm, it's the objective.

The algorithm explores block counts from a contractual range controlled by the iter_divider_start and iter_divider_end parameters, together with the fixed counts {1, 2, N}. In Fast Auto mode (fast_auto_blocks=True), four preset divider pairs are combined to broaden the coverage.

Fast Auto block presets (divider_start / divider_end): Compact (20/12), Balanced (12/6), Extended (8/4), and Very Extended (6/2.5). Each preset is computed via _compute_blocks_range_contractual and the results are combined, typically yielding 5–15 distinct block counts per run.

mine_strategies_for_block_count runs three DP passes in parallel (ThreadPoolExecutor) for each block count:

  • THICKNESS: raw P95 RMSE costs.
  • THICKNESS²: normalized squared costs for balanced block weighting.
  • SYM: symmetry-adjusted costs (see §4.3).

Up to five top-k strategies per block count and per pass are collected and validated against the _validate_strategy_blocks_contract schema (contiguous coverage, correct n_blocks, valid wavelengths).

4.2 Elite Candidate Generation

_generate_elite_candidate_strategies creates local-neighborhood variants around the top-ranked initial strategies before the final robustness evaluation:
  • Wavelength mutations: each block's monitoring wavelength is shifted by one neighboring step in the scan grid.
  • Boundary shifts: block boundaries are moved by one layer between adjacent blocks while preserving total layer coverage.
All elite candidates are validated against the block contract before being added, and duplicates are rejected through block-signature hashing. This step generates up to 120 additional strategies per run.

4.4 🔴 YIELD in the DP objective — dp_yield_weight

Phase A measures the non-terminable deposition rate per layer and per wavelength and stores it in each entry. Then certus_strat_workers_pipeline.py built the map given to the DP like this:

cost_map = {l: {x["wl"]: x["cost"] for x in items} …}

🔴 The crash rate was thrown away at this line. It only served as a binary elimination threshold: a λ at 0.001% and one at 0.106% were treated identically, even though they differ by a factor of a hundred on the only quantity that compounds over the stack height.

Why the logarithm. A deposition completes if every layer completes: $\text{yield} = \prod_i (1-p_i)$, whose logarithm is additive — exactly the form a Bellman DP optimizes exactly, without approximation. It is not a proxy: $-\log(1-p)$ is a direct component of $P(\text{compliant})$, whereas the cost in nanometers posts $\rho = -0.04$ with the result.

$$ \text{cost}(\text{layer}, \lambda) \;=\; \underbrace{c_{\text{nm}}}_{\text{local error}} \;+\; w \cdot \underbrace{\bigl(-\log(1-p)\bigr)}_{\text{log of yield}} $$

The flaw, shown on four λs of a single layer — the cheapest in nanometers is also the one whose deposition never completes:

$w$ ranking, from cheapest to most expensive
0 (today) 456 (p = 100%), 454, 452, 450
1 452, 450, 454, 456
200 450, 452, 454, 456

At zero weight — today's objective — the DP ranks first the λ whose deposition never completes, because it is the cheapest in nanometers. It thus spends its budget building paths that go through an impossible layer, before a binary guillotine deletes them.

🔴 A SUM, NOT A REPLACEMENT — and two measurements dictate it. The plan proposed replacing the nanometer cost with yield alone. But 📏 on the figure of merit, the best λ of each of the 47 layers has a ZERO crash rate: a pure yield objective would equal zero on almost all paths and the DP would become degenerate. Yield reorders where it is measurable and lets the cost in nm break ties elsewhere — strictly more information, never less.

⚠️ THIS DATA JUST BECAME INFORMATIVE, AND NOT BEFORE. As long as detection was done on a perfect TMM curve, the crash rate was zero almost everywhere and this map would have been flat. Axis 4.1 was not feasible before reading noise (§2.4) and the detection rule (§2.5).
⚠️ Resolution: $p$ is estimated on $N_{runs}$ draws, so the smallest non-zero rate is $1/N_{runs}$. Below that, the map is zero and adds nothing.
⚠️ The weight is not guessed: at the 0.107% tolerance per layer, $-\log(1-p)$ is $1.07\cdot10^{-3}$ nats; to weigh as much as 0.2 nm — the order of magnitude of the measured median cost — we need $w \approx 200$. To be swept, not set. Default 0.

🔴 AND THE FINDING IS STRONGER THAN "POORLY WEIGHTED". 📏 The two bands of the figure of merit are exactly the same width — 141 points each out of 301. A uniform RMSE is therefore literally incapable of distinguishing a strategy that misses the stop band from a strategy that misses the passband: the two scores are equal to machine precision. It is not a lack of sensitivity, it is an exact indifference — whereas the requirement differs by a factor of ~500.

📌 First-order consequence: with correct zones, the 540–560 nm transition receives a zero weight — nobody controls the shape of an edge. Yet the current score is essentially an edge shift measurement (p95 edge at 9.2 points when the passband is 1.7). We then stop judging strategies on a quantity the user never asked for.

⚠️ If no zone covers the grid, the score is $+\infty$, never 0. Returning 0 would make any strategy look perfect — a zero score is the best possible. That is the typical silent failure mode of a misentered configuration.

The figure of merit file therefore carries no targets key, and the ranking continues to be on the deviation from the nominal, unweighted spectrum — computation path identical to the bit compared to before. This is an assumed choice, not an oversight, and it must be read alongside the consequence stated above: on this component, the criterion is exactly indifferent between missing the stop band and missing the passband, and the score remains dominated by the edge shift.
The day that changes, you just need to add the zones: no code to write, no flag to raise.

🔴 The original diagnostic — RMSE measured against NOMINAL, not against target

Step 1.d compares against T_nom, the transmission of the nominal thicknesses (certus_strat_robustness.py:211), and it is this array that goes as the T_target argument of compute_batch_rmse (:706-712). The RMSE is unweighted, at constant step, over the whole range.

And STRAT has never received the target: zero occurrences of targets in the whole module, whereas DESIGN carries it as {lmin, lmax, tmin, tmax, w} zones. STRAT therefore answers "which strategy best reproduces the spectrum of the designed thicknesses?", and not "which one best respects the target?".

On the example dichroic, 400–700 nm range at 1 nm step: the stop band weighs 146 points out of 301 and its requirement is < 0.1 % — a half-point deviation there is five times out of spec — but it counts exactly as much as the passband, where the same deviation is invisible.

⚠️ Distinction not to be missed. The aimed point during deposition must remain the fixed nominal: that is the very mechanism of self-compensation. Only the figure of merit that ranks the strategies must switch to the weighted target.

2026-08-05 — CVaR95 replaces P95 as the ranking functional

A quantile is the least efficient functional of a sample: it is determined by the high tail alone. Number of points that actually decide a P95:

N draws points deciding the P95 resolution on crash_rate
6 — fast mode only one (the maximum) 17% for a 5% threshold
25 one to two 4%
150 about seven 0.7%

The CVaR95 — average of the worst 5 %, expected shortfall — keeps exactly the same risk semantics but averages the tail instead of picking one point from it. At equal N, the estimator's variance is significantly lower, without a single additional draw.

rmse_cvar95 is computed alongside rmse_p95, which remains in place. robustness_score_functional = "p95" restores the previous behavior.
⚠️ CVaR95 ≥ P95 by construction: the scores change scale and the rankings produced by the two are not comparable.

4.6 Consensus Multi-Seed Ranking (optional)

When enable_consensus_ranking=True, the top-k strategies (consensus_top_k, default 12) are re-evaluated across multiple seeds (consensus_seeds) to reduce seed-dependent outliers:

After ranking, the best strategy undergoes a final validation via _validate_strategy_min_transmission_floor. For stacks with many layers, the first few layers are critical because errors propagate cumulatively. find_robust_nucleation_wavelength_adaptive identifies a nucleation wavelength and block size that stabilize the initial layers:

10. What Makes a Good Deposition Strategy

How to read this section. It is written for a future publication, so every claim carries its register, and the two are never mixed:
  🟢 ESTABLISHED — a number produced by a run that completed, with the file it came from. It can be re-derived.
  🔵 CONJECTURE — a mechanism we believe in, together with the experiment that would refute it. It is not a result.

Unless stated otherwise, everything below is measured on the same object: the 48-layer dichroic short-pass (edge at 545 nm, H = 2.35 / L = 1.46 on BK7), seed 42, sweep grid 450–700 nm at 1 nm, 150 Monte-Carlo draws per strategy, 3 noise levels. No conclusion here is transferable to another stack (§4.9): a single component cannot separate what is physics from what is this particular design.

10.1 The quantity being maximised

There is exactly one, and it absorbs both failure modes. A run that never terminates and a filter that terminates out of specification are the same event: no usable part. So the objective is $P(\text{the part conforms})$, a scalar — which is why no Pareto front is maintained and why the ranking is not multi-criteria. Every trade-off described below is an observed consequence of that single number, not a weighting someone chose.

The reported quantity is the worst of the three noise levels — a deliberate minimax. The per-strategy figures quoted in this section are at the nominal level, and the two are never compared.

10.2 Where the control wavelengths go

The search is free to place every block anywhere on 251 grid points. What it actually converges to is far more constrained than that freedom suggests. The four statements below are counted over the full ranking (228 to 257 strategies per run, 1350 to 1570 individual block wavelengths).

The mechanism follows from what POEM is. Its anchors are inherited from earlier layers of the block, and they were acquired under an index the machine believes it knows and does not. 10.4 What is actually limiting

Two failure channels compete, and it matters which one binds.

🟢 ESTABLISHED — non-termination is not what separates good strategies from bad ones, at least at corridor 0. Split the 228 ranked strategies into those that ever failed to terminate (47) and those that never did (181):
median $\lambda$ on the edge median blocks median score
ever crashed (47) 459 nm 17 % 5 0.00793
never crashed (181) 459 nm 17 % 6 0.00788
The two populations are statistically indistinguishable on every global descriptor we can form — same median wavelength to the nanometre, same edge fraction to the percent, same spectral score to 0.6 %.

The consequence is a design decision, not a curiosity. If crashing were predictable from the strategy's summary statistics, a cheap filter would replace the Monte-Carlo screen. It is not. Non-termination is a local property — one layer, at one wavelength, under one draw — and averaging it over a strategy erases it. That is the whole argument for reporting the critical layer (§2.12) rather than a strategy-level crash rate.
🟠 MEASURED 2026-08-11 — the poorest-swing layer is a real signal, and not a sufficient one. The per-layer crash profile and the per-layer optical swing were both being computed and both being averaged away. Recovering them costs no extra run, and the first case examined looked decisive: on one winning strategy the layer with the poorest swing (0.0874, layer 39) was exactly the single layer where the stopping level became unreachable. Ten strategies in a row said the same.

The full statistic then corrects the enthusiasm: 157 out of 561, or 28 %. Against a chance baseline of 1/48 ≈ 2 %, that is a 13× enrichment — a genuine signal. But the poorest-swing layer is the failing layer only one time in four.

🔑 Which is precisely the argument for measuring the margin instead. The swing is the best cheap proxy available for "how exposed is this layer", and it explains a quarter of the failures. The margin — how far the layer actually was from crossing, measured on the simulated noisy signal rather than assumed from the clean one — is the quantity itself. A 28 % proxy is the strongest available reason to go and get it.

🟢 ESTABLISHED — no strategy that ever crashed reaches the top 20. The worst crash rate anywhere in the top 50 is 0.02 = 3 draws out of 150, and crash rates are quantised in units of $1/N$, which is worth remembering when reading small values. Exactly one strategy was eliminated outright on crash grounds.

🟢 ESTABLISHED — POEM is what makes the ranking informative at all. This is the strongest single result in the campaign and it is qualitative, not quantitative. Repeat the corridor-0.005 run with POEM disabled and absolute-level monitoring restored:
  • 87/87 strategies crash, against 54/257 with POEM.
  • SEEL degrades from 0.6 nm to 22.3 nm — a factor 37.
  • The score collapses onto a plateau: 0.2432 for 3, 4, 5 and 7 blocks, 0.2428 for 2 and 6. The ranking no longer separates.
  • All 20 best strategies place every block at the same wavelength: 517 or 522 nm, intra-strategy spread exactly 0 nm.
Without self-referenced stopping, the search stops exploring: it retreats to one safe wavelength for everything and the statistics can no longer tell the candidates apart. POEM does not merely improve the result — it is the precondition for the optimisation problem being non-degenerate.

⚠️ The protection factor proper (a ratio of costs) requires a POEM-off run at corridor 0 on the corrected code, which has not been done. The figures ×7.6 and ×6.85 quoted in earlier revisions predate the normalisation fix and must not be cited.

10.5 Can the candidate pool be pruned? No — and the measurement is worth the space

A ranking of 228 strategies is expensive, and only a handful matter: at zero corridor, 10 strategies fall in the equivalence class and only 9 of 228 are within 1.2× of the winner. The rest trail off by up to a factor 170. The obvious economy is to stop generating what will not win. It does not survive contact with the data.

At zero corridor the equivalence class is beautifully uniform — all 10 members have 2 blocks and come from two generators, ELITE and SYM. That is a pruning rule anyone would adopt. Now change the regime:

regime blocks in the class generators that produce it
corridor 0 2 ELITE, SYM
corridor 0.001 3 LOCAL_SEARCH alone
corridor 0.0025 2 – 5 ELITE, LOCAL_SEARCH, SMART_MERGE
corridor 0.005 5 LOCAL_SEARCH alone
corridor 0.010 8 LOCAL_SEARCH alone
seed 77 / 101 / 202 2 – 3 all four / LS+SMM / ELITE
🔴 LOCAL_SEARCH produces nothing at all at zero corridor, and produces the sole winner at three of the four corridor settings. The rule "drop LOCAL_SEARCH, it never wins" — which the zero-corridor data fully justifies — would have discarded the winner in four configurations out of eight. The same holds for "keep only 2-block strategies": correct at zero corridor, catastrophic at 0.010.

Every generator earns its place in at least one regime, and none earns it in all of them. A pruning rule inferred from one regime is not a saving, it is a silent loss — and it would be invisible, because the ranking would still look perfectly reasonable.

🟢 The lever that does work is depth, not population. Screening at 10 draws instead of 25 returns 133 ranked strategies instead of 228 and finds the same winner; keeping 30 survivors returns 445 and finds nothing better. So the right economy is a reallocation:

generate wide (228+) — the only protection against regime dependence
screen shallow (10 draws) — measured lossless
go deep on the finalists only (~10) — where the decision actually is

Measured cost at the final stage: ~0.15 s per (strategy × draw), with Phase A (~730 s) not needing to be redone. Spending 3000 draws on 10 finalists is about an hour — and it is the only way to measure a yield that reads 0/150 for every one of them.

10.6 The unit the results are reported in

A spectral RMSE in transmission points is not a quantity anyone has intuition for. Every result above is therefore also given as SEEL — the statistical equivalent error per layer: the Gaussian thickness error $\sigma$ which, applied independently to all 48 layers, reproduces the same spectral RMSE. It converts a score into nanometres.

🟢 ESTABLISHED — the conversion is linear, and that is measured, not assumed. Perturbing every layer at six values of $\sigma$ spanning a factor 40, three repeats each:

$$\text{RMSE} \;=\; \frac{\sigma}{k}, \qquad k = 95.4, \qquad \text{log–log slope} = \mathbf{0.989} \;\; (\sigma \in [0.05,\,2.0]\ \text{nm})$$

The single-parameter fit reproduces every point to within ±9 % across the whole range. Linearity over a factor 40 also says something physical: over the practical range the errors combine without saturation — no threshold, no runaway. SEEL is quoted quantised to 0.1 nm, which is deliberate: the third digit of a Monte-Carlo score is not information.
Reference point: the corridor-0 winner scores 0.3 nm; at the model corridor of 0.005 the best achievable becomes 0.6 nm. Index uncertainty alone doubles the effective per-layer error.

10.7 How many Monte-Carlo draws are actually needed

The question has a number, and the number changes what the ranking should do. At the operating depth of 150 draws, a strategy's score wobbles by ±5 to 6 % from sampling alone — measured two independent ways, and the 1/√N scaling is exact (12.56 % at N = 32 against 6.28 % at N = 128, a ratio of 2.00 for a fourfold depth).

The gap between the winner and the runner-up is 6.5 %. Two independent scores differ with σ√2 ≈ 8 %. They are 0.8 σ apart. What it would cost to separate them properly:

to resolve draws per strategy
a 6.5 % score gap at 3σ ~2 000 (×13)
a 5 % vs 10 % failure rate ~430
0.1 % vs 0.5 % ~2 900
🔑 So the answer is not to buy score resolution — it is to reallocate. Thirteen times the compute to conclude "they are equivalent" is money spent to learn what the equivalence class already says. The measured cost is ~0.15 s per (strategy × draw) at the final stage, with Phase A (~730 s) not repeated. So:
generate wide — 228+, the only guard against regime dependence
screen shallow — 10 draws, measured lossless
go deep on ~10 finalists — 3000 draws, about an hour
Deep on ten is affordable; deep on 228 is not. And on those ten the useful question is no longer the score — it is the yield, which reads 0/150 for every one of them.

⚠️ Two cautions on going deeper. First, never rank on which strategy crashes first: the draw count to a first event is geometric, so its standard deviation equals its mean — 100 % dispersion, trading a 6 % noise for a 100 % one. Use a common budget and compare confidence intervals. Second, some strategies will never crash at all: the perturbations are bounded, so once the margin exceeds the largest possible perturbation the probability is exactly zero, not small. No number of draws produces an event. That is why the margin comes first — it says, without a single draw, which strategies can even fail.

10.8 Open conjectures, and the experiment that settles each

These are not results. Each is stated with its refutation condition, so that a reader can hold us to it.

🔵 CONJECTURE the decisive experiment
1. Spread beats grouping because a crossed dispersion curve is locally compensable. A shifted curve is corrected identically everywhere, so one monitoring wavelength suffices; a crossed curve has opposite-signed error either side of the crossing, so a correction made at one $\lambda$ actively worsens the other side. Two runs at the same corridor, one with $b = 0$ (pure shift), one with $a = 0$ (pure crossing). If the conjecture holds, the block count grows with $b$ and stays flat with $a$. Not yet run.
2. The critical layer is identifiable and it is not the thinnest. We expect it to be the layer with the poorest optical swing at its assigned $\lambda$, not the one with the smallest physical thickness. Record the stopping margin per (draw, layer, cause) instead of collapsing on the layer axis, then check the identified layer is stable when the noise level is doubled. A critical layer that moves with the noise level is an artefact of the estimator, not a property of the design.
3. Monochromator resolution is part of the strategy, not a machine setting. A wider slit lowers the noise but biases the expected level, because the controller computes its targets at perfect resolution. The optimum should therefore depend on where the blocks are. Sweep the four available slits at fixed strategy, then re-optimise the strategy at each slit. If the winning strategy is slit-independent, the conjecture is wrong and resolution is a machine setting after all.
4. Low-swing layers should be deposited in Rate mode, and the optimiser should choose that per layer. Below a swing threshold there is no usable photometric signal, and turn-counting with no feedback may beat a trigger that fires on noise. The rate error is not a free parameter — the simulator knows both the real and nominal thicknesses of every previous layer of the same material, so it can estimate the rate the way the machine does. Measure it; do not posit it.
5. Ranking depth beyond ~25 draws buys robustness, not a different winner. At 25 draws the winner is already id 2228, the same as at 150. Read the dispersion of nested sub-packets inside one deep run rather than repeating shallow runs. Sobol is nested, so packets of 100 drawn from 500 are refinements of the same sequence, not fresh samples — the sub-packet spread is the answer.

10.9 A second test component — and why it is not a second data point

Every figure above comes from one stack. Since 2026-08-11 a second one exists: a three-cavity bandpass, M5·2L·M5·L·M5·2L·M5·L·M5·2L·M5, 35 layers, centred at 632.02 nm with a 15.7 nm passband, measured over 600–660 nm. Same materials, same monochromator sweep (450–700 nm) — because the sweep range is a property of the instrument, not of the design.

🔑 It asks a different question of the method, which is the point of having it. Its layers are quarter-waves at $\lambda_0$, so each one stops at a turning point where $dT/dd \to 0$ — the regime §14 warns against, and the one a bandpass is normally monitored in (turning-point monitoring). The dichroic, by contrast, lives on intermediate levels. And its spectral ripple near the control wavelength is 5.0 nm against 7.2 nm for the dichroic, so a 2 nm slit averages over a larger fraction of a ripple: it is the more exposed of the two.

⚠️ Two stacks do not make a validation, and the addition changes nothing about §15. Both are coherence benches; neither has a real deposition behind it. What a second component buys is falsification of transfer: it shows whether a conclusion drawn on one survives the other. It never licenses carrying a number across.

🔴 And the geometry of the target is not the same, which must be known before any two scores are set side by side. The dichroic has 141 points per band out of 301 — exactly equal. The bandpass has roughly 15 in-band points out of 61. The two put a different question to the same unweighted objective, so a score gap between them is not a difficulty gap.
🔑 A trap worth recording, because anyone would fall into it. A three-cavity bandpass has a flat, rippled top — here $T > 0.99$ over 10.4 nm. Centring it on argmax is meaningless: the maximum hops between ripples depending on the evaluation grid, and the same filter was reported peaking at 637.0 nm on one grid and 627.1 nm on another. The quantity that centres a bandpass is the midpoint of the half-maximum band, on which the relation is monotone and a bisection converges.

10.10 What we deliberately do not claim

  • None of this is externally validated. STRAT is currently checked only against itself. The dichroic is a coherence bench, not an independent referee. The only closure is real deposition: two strategies actually deposited, spectra measured, and STRAT required to rank them in the correct order — an ordinal test, far weaker than absolute agreement and far more convincing than self-consistency.
  • No conclusion is transferred to another stack. Every figure here is the 48-layer dichroic. The three-cavity bandpass of §10.9 exists to test whether a conclusion survives the change of component — never to carry one across.
  • The spectral target is unweighted, and this is a stated choice. Its consequence must be reported alongside any result: the two bands of this design are the same width — 141 points out of 301 each — so a uniform RMSE is literally unable to prefer a strategy that misses the stopband over one that misses the passband, though the requirements differ by orders of magnitude. The score is dominated by edge displacement, which nobody asked for.
  • The literature heuristics are diagnostics, not filters. The 15–85 % window, the 4 % minimum starting amplitude, swing-in/swing-out: reported as explanatory columns, never used to reject a candidate. Working from simulated noisy signals removes the need for the rules of thumb those signals were unavailable to justify.
  • An unmeasured mechanism is not modelled. No $\sigma(T)$, no shot noise, no multiplicative noise, no inter-revolution correlation. The reading noise model has one measured number and no free parameter; adding structure nobody measured would trade a measured constant for invented ones.

11. Making It Fast — What the Structure Gives for Free

Where the time actually goes, measured: a fixed ~730 s (Phase A plus the DP) and ~0.15 s per (strategy × draw) at the final stage. Phase A is therefore 68 % of a reference run, and it is dominated by one function — the growth kernel. Every optimisation below targets that; nothing else is worth touching.

11.1 T(d) has a closed form, and it is exact

For the layer currently growing on an already-deposited stack, the transmission denominator is C·cosδ + i·S·sinδ with C and S constant — they depend on the stack below, not on the thickness being deposited. Expanding the modulus turns it into a pure sinusoid:

$$T(d) = \frac{4\,n_{\text{sub}}}{P + Q\cos 2\delta + R\sin 2\delta}, \qquad \delta = \frac{2\pi n d}{\lambda}$$

📏 Verified numerically to 5.4×10−20. This is not an approximation — it is the same computation written differently. P, Q and R are computed once from the stack matrix, after which any thickness costs two trigonometric calls and no complex arithmetic at all.

And the derivatives come free. Because the denominator is a pure sinusoid,

$$\frac{d^2 D}{d\delta^2} = -4\,(D - P)$$

🔑 the second derivative needs no new trigonometric evaluation — it follows from the value already computed. What this replaces:

today closed form
64 complex 2×2 matrix products per layer ×1.20 measured on the compiled kernel
find turning points by scanning tan 2δ = R/Q — closed form
find the stop by scanning then interpolating closed form
3 TMM evaluations for the parabolic inversion 1 evaluation plus exact derivatives
the slit-bias curvature (3 TMM per layer) analytic
🔴 The limit, and how it was nearly got wrong twice. The form assumes a real δ, hence k = 0. Two earlier attempts to locate that limit used random stack matrices, whose denominator can approach zero — T then explodes and the error gets measured on unphysical values. Those attempts reported the form breaking at k = 10−3, then at 10−6. Both were artefacts of the test rig.

On a real 24-layer stack, keeping only physical transmissions, the error grows linearly in k and stays four decades below the reading noise: 4.2×10−8 at k = 10−4, which is 0.008 % of the noise amplitude. Restricting STRAT to k < 10−4 is therefore comfortable, with a decade of margin.

⚠️ The guard must raise, never silently fall back — and the fast path must be validated against the independent TMM oracle, not against the kernel. Two sign bugs worth 46 and 82 points of reflectance have already hidden inside re-implementations, and both were exact at k = 0: invisible to any test that only looks at dielectrics, which is precisely this case.
🔴 AND THE SPEED-UP IS ×1.20, NOT ×3.6 — a correction owed. The ×3.6 was measured in pure Python, where every complex operation goes through the interpreter. The kernel is numba-compiled: there the matrix product is already machine code, and swapping eight complex multiplications for two trigonometric calls changes little — cos and sin are not free either. Clean A/B at equal physics (k = 10−5 against 10−3): 0.276 s against 0.331 s. A Python benchmark does not predict a compiled kernel.

🔴 And three follow-on ideas were measured and closed.
  • Replacing the parabolic inversion: the parabola is already exact to 2.1×10−10 nm over 200 random stacks — 240 million times below the 0.05 nm at which a thickness difference stops meaning anything. It also explains a figure from §2.8: POEM's invariance measured at 2.19×10−10 nm was the parabola's limit, not POEM's.
  • Analytic dT/dd: outside the hot loop, and changing it would move the noise scale — hence every result — for no gain.
  • 🔴 Solving the turning points analytically: structurally impossible. The detector runs on the noisy signal and is a hysteresis machine, not an extremum finder: it models what the instrument believes it sees. An analytic extremum of the clean curve would delete the false-turning-point mechanism entirely — 79 % of the measured failures.

11.2 The scan window — a negative result, reported as one

The sweep runs from zero to three times the nominal thickness. 📏 Measured: 63 % of it covers thicknesses no layer will ever reach without being scrap — on a 253 nm layer it scans out to 760 nm.

🔑 And the defect is not that 3 is too large — it is the scaling. The window is a multiple of the layer thickness, while both reasons to go beyond the nominal are fixed in nanometres: the largest meaningful thickness error, and the half optical period λ/4n — 57.9 nm on H, 93.2 nm on L, independent of how thick the layer is. A multiple is thus too generous on a thick layer and possibly too short on a thin one: the same parameter wrong in both directions.

The claim that constant sampling density made this physics-neutral was wrong. The density is preserved, but the reachability test bounds its window at the next extremum after the stop, and a shorter sweep no longer contains one — it falls back on the end of the array, which is more permissive. The shortened sweep was hiding crashes: the worst possible direction for an error.

🔑 What the failure teaches is worth more than the speed would have been. The sweep serves three purposes, not one: finding the stop wants a short window finely sampled; detecting turning points and bounding reachability want a long window whose density hardly matters. A two-zone sweep would serve all three — but only once the TMM grid and the noise grid are separated, because until then a non-uniform sampling means a non-uniform draw density, and the draw count is what governs how often noise fabricates a false extremum.

11.3 Single precision — defensible, but not first

The quantity carries a reading noise of A = 5×10−4. A float32 relative error of ~10−7 is 5×10−8 in absolute terms on transmissions of order 0.5 — four decades below the noise, and still around 10−6 after accumulating over 48 matrix products.

What it costs is not accuracy but verifiability: bit-level comparison becomes impossible, and the independent TMM oracle drops from 3.3×10−16 to ~10−7 — four decades of sensitivity lost on the very instrument that caught the two sign bugs. Monitoring path only, never the scoring path and never the oracle.

⚠️ And after the closed form, never alongside it. The closed form changes what the bottleneck is — the work stops being complex matrix products and becomes trigonometry — so where the time goes must be re-measured before deciding what to demote.
