Overcoming Photometric Amplitude

The Topological Revolution: Why the pure spectral localization of extrema historically outclasses the Swanepoel envelope method.

The Swanepoel Limit

The classic Swanepoel method (1983) extracts the refractive index $n$ and the extinction coefficient $k$ by postulating that the amplitudes of the extrema ($T_{max}$, $T_{min}$) are exact and free of defects.

However, this is an experimental fallacy. Transmission amplitude levels are heavily altered by atomic roughness (scattering effect), sample holder parallelism, spectral convolution due to detector resolution (slit width), and photomultiplier non-linearity.

Result: an amplitude loss of purely geometric or material origin is immediately – and falsely – interpreted cartographically by Swanepoel as a severe increase in the absorption parameter (a false $k$) and a distortion of the index $n$.

The Topological Paradigm

The new CERTUS approach completely ignores peak amplitude. It exploits the only physically unshakable information: the spectral abscissa ($\lambda$) of interference maxima and minima.

Even if the actual amplitude of a fringe is blurred, destroyed, or halved by a spectrophotometer slit or surface scattering (Rayleigh), the central position of its zero derivative $\frac{dT_{rel}}{d\lambda} = 0$ remains mathematically invariant.

  • Stability to intensity variations
  • Independence from spectral detection resolution
  • Utilization of grating calibration (accuracy $< 0.1$ nm)

Fundamental Conclusion

The observed variance on the final refractive index and the extracted thickness demonstrates that even with an enormous and unrealistic noise of 0.5 nm on the fringe positions, the physics of the thickness is preserved. Swanepoel would have exploded at the slightest blur on the amplitudes, whereas here, the thickness varies by 0.985% (max. relative standard deviation on \(d\), all conditions) and the profile index by 0.0250 (max. standard deviation on \(n_1\)). This topological firing protocol formally validates this approach as the worthy and more robust successor to Swanepoel for the ultra-precise characterization of thick materials.
