CERTUS-METAL SINGLE

Single Layer Extraction on Transparent Substrate

Scientific Revision 2026.03

Executive Summary: CERTUS-METAL SINGLE determines the complex refractive index of a single metallic or absorbing layer deposited directly onto a transparent substrate (Silica or BK7). It leverages multiple optical signatures (Reflectance, Transmission, and Back-side Reflectance) to uniquely constrain the solution, allowing for the simultaneous extraction of thickness ($d_M$) and spline-based optical constants ($n, k$) without physical model assumptions.

1. Context: The "Single Layer" Challenge

Characterizing a single absorbing layer on a transparent substrate provides more information channels than an opaque bilayer stack. Since the substrate is transparent:

  • Transmission ($T$): Light passes through the substrate, offering powerful absorption constraints ($k$).
  • Back-Reflectance ($R_{back}$): Measured through the substrate, probing the substrate-film interface.
  • Front-Reflectance ($R$): Probes the air-film interface.

Combining $\{R, T, R_{back}\}$ creates a highly constrained optimization landscape, reducing degeneracy compared to $R$-only measurements.

2. Theoretical Model

2.1. Layer Structure

The physical model is:

Air | Metal ($d_M$, Spline $n,k$) | Transparent Substrate (Incoherent)

Incoherent Substrate Modeling: The substrate (typically ~0.5–1 mm thick) is much thicker than the coherence length of light. Interference fringes inside the substrate are therefore averaged out. The model analytically sums intensities rather than amplitudes for the substrate traversing paths.
  • Substrate: Selectable Fused Silica or BK7 (Sellmeier dispersion).
  • Metal Film: Modeled with free-form cubic splines for $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$.

2.2. Transfer Matrix Method (Macleod Convention)

The characteristic matrix for the single metal layer uses the Macleod convention ($\hat{n} = n - ik$, factor +1j):

$$ \mathbf{M}_{metal} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi & \frac{+i\sin\phi}{\hat{n}} \\ +i\hat{n}\sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\hat{n}\, d_M $$

2.3. Incoherent Substrate Combination

The front-surface TMM gives coherent $R_{front}$, $T_{front}$. The substrate back-surface reflects with Fresnel intensity coefficient:

$$ R_{ext} = \left(\frac{n_{sub} - 1}{n_{sub} + 1}\right)^2, \qquad T_{ext} = 1 - R_{ext} $$

The total observables are computed by the exact incoherent sum (geometric series):

$$ T_{total} = \frac{T_{front} \cdot T_{ext}}{1 - R_{back} \cdot R_{ext}}, \qquad R_{total} = R_{front} + \frac{T_{front}^2 \cdot R_{ext}}{1 - R_{back} \cdot R_{ext}} $$

$R_{back}$ is computed from a reverse matrix (Sub→Air), ensuring correct treatment even for absorbing films where $R_{front} \neq R_{back}$.

2.4. Spline-Based Material Model

The optical constants are reconstructed as free-form curves:

  • Knots: Adaptive control points $(n_i, k_i)$ at wavelengths $\lambda_i$.
  • Natural Cubic Splines: $C^2$-continuous interpolation between knots.
  • Physics Constraints: $n > 0$, $k \ge 0$.
  • Knot Distance Constraint: Minimum spacing $\Delta\lambda_{min}$ prevents over-fitting.

3.1. Optimization Vector

The algorithm searches for:

  • $d_M$: Metal thickness (e.g., 20 nm)
  • $\{n_i, k_i\}$: Optical constants at $N$ knot positions.
  • $\{\lambda_i\}_{i=2}^{N-1}$: Spectral position of internal knots (adaptive to features).

3.2. Multi-Channel Cost Function

The total error is the average of errors from all available data channels:

$$ \text{MSE}_{total} = \frac{1}{N_{ch}} \sum_{c \in \{R,T,R_{back}\}} \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \left(S_c^{calc}(\lambda_j) - S_c^{meas}(\lambda_j)\right)^2 $$

This "triangulation" forces the solution to satisfy physical reality at both interfaces and the bulk absorption simultaneously. Missing channels are automatically excluded.

3.3. Differential Evolution

The global search uses Differential Evolution (DE) — a population-based stochastic optimizer ideal for high-dimensional, non-convex problems:

  1. Initialize: Population of random parameter vectors within bounds.
  2. Mutate: Trial vectors via weighted differences: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{x}_{r1} + F(\mathbf{x}_{r2} - \mathbf{x}_{r3})$
  3. Crossover: Mix trial and target vectors with probability CR.
  4. Select: Keep the better of trial and target based on MSE.

Typical parameters: Population = 50, $F \in [0.5, 1.0]$, CR = 0.7, Max iterations = 1500.

4. Beam Analysis: Uncertainty Quantification

4.1. The Uniqueness Check

While the combination of $R, T, R_{back}$ highly constrains the solution, experimental noise can still create ambiguity between thickness and refractive index. The Beam Analysis post-processing step systematically maps this uncertainty.

4.2. Scan Methodology

  1. Reference: Starts from the optimal thickness $d_{opt}$ found by the main solver.
  2. Constraint Scan: Step thickness in both directions ($\pm 1$ nm steps). At each step, fix $d$ and re-optimize the spline optical constants to find the best possible match.
  3. Valley Mapping: Plot $\text{MSE}_{min}$ vs. Thickness.

4.3. Interpretation

  • V-Shape: A sharp V confirms a unique solution.
  • Flat Bottom: Indicates parameter correlation (uncertainty in $d$ vs $n$).
  • Beam Envelope: The software exports the bundle of all $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$ curves whose MSE is within a tolerance (e.g., 2%) of the global minimum. This provides process windows technically rigorous for production control.

4.4. Uncertainty Formula

The spectral uncertainty envelope is computed as:

$$ \Delta n(\lambda) = \max_d n_{sol}(\lambda) - \min_d n_{sol}(\lambda), \qquad \text{for all } d \text{ where } \text{MSE}(d) \le \text{MSE}_{opt} \cdot (1 + \tau) $$

5. Usage Guide

5.1. Input Data Format

CSV or Excel file with up to 4 columns (auto-detected):

Column Content Unit
1 Wavelength ($\lambda$) nm
2 Reflectance ($R$) 0–1 or %
3 (Optional) Transmission ($T$) 0–1 or %
4 (Optional) Back-Reflectance ($R_{back}$) 0–1 or %

5.2. Configuration Guidelines

  • Knots: Start with 2–3 for simple metals. Increase only if fit is poor.
  • Thickness Range: Constrain $d_M$ (min/max) to ±20% of target deposition.
  • Substrate: Correct substrate selection (Silica vs BK7) is critical as it affects $R_{back}$ and $T$ baselines significantly.

6. Output & Reporting

  • Visualization Tabs:
    • Spectra: Target vs Calculated $R$, $T$, $R_{back}$.
    • n & k: Resulting optical constants with knots visualization.
    • Convergence: Log-scale RMSE trace.
