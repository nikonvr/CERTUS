CERTUS-METAL BILAYER

Spline-based optical constant extraction and beam analysis

Implementation review: 2026-10-03

Abstract: CERTUS-METAL BILAYER characterizes opaque or semi-transparent metallic layers deposited on a dielectric buffer such as SiO$_2$. The method reconstructs the complex refractive index ($\hat{n} = n - ik$) with free-form cubic splines and includes a beam-analysis stage to map the thickness–index ambiguity.

1. Measurement Context and Identifiability

Reflectance-only inversion of an absorbing film can admit several thickness and optical-constant combinations with similar spectra. The result therefore depends on the measured wavelength range, the parameter bounds, the spline representation, and the chosen fit criterion.

CERTUS-METAL BILAYER fits spline optical constants and offers a local thickness-profile diagnostic. Neither stage establishes uniqueness from reflectance alone.

2. Theoretical Model

2.1 Layer Structure

The model assumes a standardized test structure for characterization:

Air | Metal (Unknown) | SiO₂ (Cauchy) | Si (Tabulated)

  • Silicon substrate: uses tabulated $n,k$ data through get_nk_si; fitting should stay inside the range supported by the data table.
  • SiO₂ Buffer: Modeled with Cauchy dispersion:
    $$ n_{SiO_2}(\lambda) = n_\infty + \frac{A}{\lambda^2} $$
    with configurable bounds on $n_\infty$ and $A$.
  • Metal Film: Modeled with free-form cubic splines for $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$.

2.2 Transfer Matrix Method (Macleod Convention)

For each layer at wavelength $\lambda$, the characteristic matrix uses the Macleod convention with complex refractive index $\hat{n} = n - ik$ (k > 0) and factor +1j in off-diagonal elements:

$$ \mathbf{M}_j = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi_j & \frac{+i\sin\phi_j}{\hat{n}_j} \\ +i\hat{n}_j\sin\phi_j & \cos\phi_j \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi_j = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\hat{n}_j d_j $$

The total system matrix for the bilayer is built by pre-multiplication (Air→Substrate):

$$ \mathbf{M}_{sys} = \mathbf{M}_{Metal} \cdot \mathbf{M}_{SiO_2} = \begin{pmatrix} m_{00} & m_{01} \\ m_{10} & m_{11} \end{pmatrix} $$

The amplitude reflection coefficient at normal incidence ($n_0 = 1$, $n_S$ = Si complex index):

$$ r = \frac{m_{00} + n_S\,m_{01} - m_{10} - n_S\,m_{11}}{m_{00} + n_S\,m_{01} + m_{10} + n_S\,m_{11}}, \qquad R = |r|^2 $$
Note on Opaque Films: When the metal layer is optically thick ($\alpha d \gg 1$), the transmitted signal becomes difficult to measure. Reflectance alone may then poorly identify thickness and optical constants; the optional Beam Analysis (§5) samples one local family of compensating fits.

2.3 Cubic Spline Interpolation

The optical constants $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$ are constructed from natural cubic splines:

  • A set of $N$ knots at wavelengths $\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_N\}$ with values $\{n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_N\}$ and $\{k_1, k_2, \ldots, k_N\}$.
  • Boundary condition: second derivatives vanish at endpoints (natural splines).
  • Knot values are bounded by configurable $n,k$ limits (default $[0,10]$).

Natural cubic interpolation is $C^2$ continuous; the objective adds a separate curvature penalty to discourage oscillations.

Knot Distance Constraint: A minimum spacing $\Delta\lambda_{min}$ is enforced between adjacent knots to prevent over-fitting and ensure numerical stability.

3. Optimization Architecture

3.1 Parameter Vector

The optimization searches over a vector $\mathbf{x}$ containing:

Parameter Description Bounds
$d_M$ Metal thickness User-defined (e.g., 15–35 nm)
$d_L$ SiO₂ thickness Nominal ± variation
$n_\infty, A$ Cauchy parameters for SiO₂ Configurable bounds
$\{n_i\}_{i=1}^N$ n values at knot positions $[n_{min}, n_{max}]$
$\{k_i\}_{i=1}^N$ k values at knot positions $[k_{min}, k_{max}]$
$\{\lambda_i\}_{i=2}^{N-1}$ Internal knot positions Wavelength range

3.2 Objective Function

_bilayer_reflectance_mse compares calculated and measured reflectance, with a curvature penalty on the reconstructed metal $n$ and $k$ curves:

$$ J = \operatorname{mean}_j\left(R_{calc}(\lambda_j)-R_{meas}(\lambda_j)\right)^2 +10^{-2}\left[\sum_j(\Delta^2 n_j)^2+\sum_j(\Delta^2 k_j)^2\right]. $$

Invalid thicknesses, non-finite values, and knot-spacing violations are rejected. The displayed RMSE is the square root of the objective, so it includes the curvature term.

3.3 PGLOBAL with Knot Continuation and Local Polish

The worker calls run_pglobal_optimization, which wraps the native PGlobalOptimizer. Unless an initial vector is supplied, the worker fits $K=2$ through the requested knot count in sequence. Each stage explores a bounded parameter space and passes its best vector to the next stage. Bounded L-BFGS-B restarts without a supplied analytic Jacobian polish the final vector; the separate beam refit uses an analytic gradient. This stochastic search returns a best-found fit; it does not certify a global optimum.

Implementation summary Global fit: knot continuation and native PGLOBAL minimize reflectance error plus spline curvature, with bounded L-BFGS-B restarts on the final solution. Optional beam diagnostic: a fixed-metal-thickness scan refits buffer thickness, its two Cauchy parameters, and the metal spline values and internal knot positions. The retained curves describe this local fit family under the implemented threshold; they are not a calibrated confidence region.

5. Beam Analysis: Local Thickness-Profile Diagnostic

BeamAnalysisWorker.run_beam_analysis starts at the fitted metal thickness and scans outward in both directions with a default 0.5 nm step. At each fixed metal thickness, bounded L-BFGS-B refits the remaining vector: SiO₂ thickness, $n_\infty$, $A$, metal $n/k$ knot values, and internal knot wavelengths. It uses an analytic objective/gradient pair and retries with relaxed tolerances if needed.

Accepted solutions satisfy the implementation's heuristic threshold:

$$ D=\{d_M\text{ sampled}\mid J(d_M)\leq \max(1.25J_{opt}+10^{-5},\,2\times10^{-5})\}. $$

The displayed $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$ envelopes are minima and maxima over retained refits. They show sensitivity to the local thickness scan, bounds, optimizer basin, and threshold. They are not statistically calibrated confidence intervals and do not establish parameter uniqueness.

6. Robust Polishing Strategy

The bilayer objective is nonlinear; local polishing can stop in different basins.

For polishing and beam exploration, CERTUS-METAL BILAYER uses a cascaded strategy:

  1. Beam refit: uses an analytic objective/gradient pair with bound constraints.
  2. Relaxed-tolerance fallback: if the first beam refit fails or has a high objective, it retries L-BFGS-B with relaxed tolerances.

7. Key Advantages

7.1 Empirical Metal Dispersion

The metal spline fits the measured response without imposing a Drude model. Its curvature penalty and bounds still constrain the fitted curves; measurements outside the fitted band require separate validation.

7.2 Thickness Sensitivity

The beam output shows the range of $n$ and $k$ among local fits retained by the chosen objective threshold. It should be reported with the scan range and threshold, not as a calibrated uncertainty bound.

8. Interface and Reporting

The fit and optional beam analysis run in workers and report progress to the GUI.

8.1 Controls

Use the window's Load, Run, Stop, Save and Beam controls. Check the running window for active keyboard bindings; a command label alone does not establish a working shortcut.

8.2 Dynamic Processing Feedback

The worker emits progress, current best fit and completion events; the main window updates its plots and export controls from those events.
