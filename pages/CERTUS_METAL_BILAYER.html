CERTUS-METAL BILAYER

Spline-based optical constant extraction and beam analysis

Scientific report revision 2026.04.17

Abstract: CERTUS-METAL BILAYER characterizes opaque or semi-transparent metallic layers deposited on a dielectric buffer such as SiO$_2$. The method reconstructs the complex refractive index ($\hat{n} = n - ik$) with free-form cubic splines and includes a beam-analysis stage to map the thickness–index ambiguity.

1. The Scientific Problem: The Rigid Model Trap

Standard characterization of metals often fails in production because evaporated layers are not perfect crystals. They exhibit surface roughness and density gradients that violate simple Drude laws. Furthermore, for opaque layers, an infinite number of $(n, k, d)$ combinations can produce the same Reflectance spectrum.

CERTUS-METAL BILAYER solves this by abandoning rigid formulas in favor of Adaptive Knots and performing a systematic exploration of the solution space.

2. Theoretical Model

2.1 Layer Structure

The model assumes a standardized test structure for characterization:

Air | Metal (Unknown) | SiO₂ (Cauchy) | Si (Tabulated)

  • Silicon Substrate: Uses tabulated $n,k$ data (200–1000 nm) with interpolation.
  • SiO₂ Buffer: Modeled with Cauchy dispersion:
    $$ n_{SiO_2}(\lambda) = n_\infty + \frac{A}{\lambda^2} + \frac{B}{\lambda^4} $$
    constrained to physically valid bounds (e.g. $1.44 \leq n \leq 1.475$).
  • Metal Film: Modeled with free-form cubic splines for $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$.

2.2 Transfer Matrix Method (Macleod Convention)

For each layer at wavelength $\lambda$, the characteristic matrix uses the Macleod convention with complex refractive index $\hat{n} = n - ik$ (k > 0) and factor +1j in off-diagonal elements:

$$ \mathbf{M}_j = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi_j & \frac{+i\sin\phi_j}{\hat{n}_j} \\ +i\hat{n}_j\sin\phi_j & \cos\phi_j \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi_j = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\hat{n}_j d_j $$

The total system matrix for the bilayer is built by pre-multiplication (Air→Substrate):

$$ \mathbf{M}_{sys} = \mathbf{M}_{Metal} \cdot \mathbf{M}_{SiO_2} = \begin{pmatrix} m_{00} & m_{01} \\ m_{10} & m_{11} \end{pmatrix} $$

The amplitude reflection coefficient at normal incidence ($n_0 = 1$, $n_S$ = Si complex index):

$$ r = \frac{m_{00} + n_S\,m_{01} - m_{10} - n_S\,m_{11}}{m_{00} + n_S\,m_{01} + m_{10} + n_S\,m_{11}}, \qquad R = |r|^2 $$
Note on Opaque Films: When the metal layer is optically thick ($\alpha d \gg 1$), the transmitted signal vanishes and only reflectance data is available. In this regime, $n$ and $k$ are strongly correlated with thickness $d$ — the Beam Analysis (§5) is essential to quantify this degeneracy.

2.3 Cubic Spline Interpolation

The optical constants $n(\lambda)$ and $k(\lambda)$ are constructed from natural cubic splines:

  • A set of $N$ knots at wavelengths $\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_N\}$ with values $\{n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_N\}$ and $\{k_1, k_2, \ldots, k_N\}$.
  • Boundary condition: second derivatives vanish at endpoints (natural splines).
  • Values are clipped to physical range: $0 \leq n, k \leq 10$.

The spline provides $C^2$ continuity, ensuring smooth dispersion curves without artificial oscillations.

Knot Distance Constraint: A minimum spacing $\Delta\lambda_{min}$ is enforced between adjacent knots to prevent over-fitting and ensure numerical stability.

3. Optimization Architecture

3.1 Parameter Vector

The optimization searches over a vector $\mathbf{x}$ containing:

Parameter Description Bounds
$d_M$ Metal thickness User-defined (e.g., 15–35 nm)
$d_L$ SiO₂ thickness Nominal ± variation
$n_\infty, A$ Cauchy parameters for SiO₂ Physical constraints
$\{n_i\}_{i=1}^N$ n values at knot positions $[n_{min}, n_{max}]$
$\{k_i\}_{i=1}^N$ k values at knot positions $[k_{min}, k_{max}]$
$\{\lambda_i\}_{i=2}^{N-1}$ Internal knot positions Wavelength range

3.2 Objective Function

$$ \text{MSE} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \left( R_{calc}(\lambda_j) - R_{meas}(\lambda_j) \right)^2 $$

The optimization minimizes this MSE subject to physical constraints (positive thicknesses, valid Cauchy indices).

3.3 Hybrid Optimization Strategy

Stage 1: Differential Evolution (Global Search)

Differential Evolution (DE) is a population-based stochastic optimizer suitable for non-convex problems.

  1. Initialize: generate a population of parameter vectors within the bounds.
  2. Mutate: build trial vectors using weighted differences: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{x}_{r1} + F(\mathbf{x}_{r2} - \mathbf{x}_{r3})$
  3. Crossover: combine trial and target vectors with probability CR.
  4. Select: retain the vector with the lower MSE.
  5. Iterate: repeat until convergence or the maximum number of iterations is reached.

Typical parameters are: population size 50, $F \in [0.5, 1.0]$, CR = 0.7, and a maximum of 1500 iterations.

Stage 2: Local Polishing

After the global search, candidate solutions are refined with local optimizers. L-BFGS-B with analytic gradients is used with warm starts from nearby solutions, with a relaxed-tolerance fallback.

5. Beam Analysis: Uncertainty Quantification

5.1 Thickness-Index Degeneracy

For absorbing layers, multiple $(d, n, k)$ combinations can produce similar spectra. The Beam Analysis systematically explores this degeneracy.

5.2 Bi-Directional Scan Algorithm

  1. Reference solution: start from the optimal thickness $d_{opt}$.
  2. Outward scan: vary the thickness in both directions using 0.5 nm steps.
  3. Re-optimize: for each fixed $d_M$, optimize only the spline parameters.
  4. Tolerance filter: retain solutions with $\text{MSE} \leq \text{MSE}_{opt} \times (1 + \tau)$.
  5. Warm start: use the neighboring solution as the initial guess when available.
Result: a beam envelope showing:
  • $n(\lambda)_{min}, n(\lambda)_{max}$ — uncertainty bounds on the refractive index.
  • $k(\lambda)_{min}, k(\lambda)_{max}$ — uncertainty bounds on the extinction coefficient.
  • All solutions with MSE within tolerance of the optimum.

5.3 Uncertainty Formula

The spectral uncertainty envelope is strictly defined by the valid perturbation domain:

$$ \Delta n(\lambda) = \max_{d \in D_\tau} n_{sol}(\lambda, d) - \min_{d \in D_\tau} n_{sol}(\lambda, d) $$ $$ \Delta k(\lambda) = \max_{d \in D_\tau} k_{sol}(\lambda, d) - \min_{d \in D_\tau} k_{sol}(\lambda, d) $$ $$ \text{with } D_\tau = \{ d \mid \text{MSE}(d) \le \text{MSE}_{opt} \cdot (1 + \tau) \} $$

5.4 Multi-Valley Detection (EnsembleWorkerPGlobal)

For complex solution landscapes, an ensemble of independent PGLOBAL runs identifies distinct valleys:

  • Eight parallel runs from different starting points.
  • Clustering (DBSCAN): groups similar solutions based on normalized parameter distance.
  • Reports the best solution from each cluster.

6. Robust Polishing Strategy

The bilayer parameter landscape is highly non-linear. Standard gradient methods may fail to reach the true minimum.

For polishing and beam exploration, CERTUS-METAL BILAYER uses a cascaded strategy:

  1. L-BFGS-B fast descent: uses exact analytic gradients for a precise descent with bound constraints.
  2. Relaxed-tolerance fallback: if the gradient-based step stalls or produces invalid parameter combinations, the system retries L-BFGS-B with relaxed ftol/gtol thresholds to bypass numerical noise.

7. Key Advantages

7.1 Model-Agnostic Fitting

Suitable for novel alloys or oxidized surfaces where standard literature values do not apply. The spline approach fits the measured response rather than imposing a rigid analytic model.

7.2 Rigorous Uncertainty Quantification

By exporting the beam envelope, researchers can estimate uncertainty bounds on the refractive index, which is relevant for high-precision optical analysis.
