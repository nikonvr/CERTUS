Envelope amplitudes and fringe positions

Both methods start from the extrema of a transmission spectrum. The measurement used at each extremum determines which calibration matters most.

How the two methods use a spectrum

MethodObservableMeasurements to check
Swanepoel envelopesTransmission at maxima and minimaIntensity calibration, scattering and spectral resolution. Assigning unmodelled loss to absorption can bias the inferred index and extinction coefficient.
Fringe positionsWavelengths of successive extrema, which constrain optical thicknessWavelength calibration, fringe order and peak-location uncertainty. Overlap and asymmetric broadening can shift a detected extremum.

The metrology guide gives the envelope equations and the associated uncertainty budget.

What the zero-noise trials establish

The mean fitted thickness lies 37.533 nm above the nominal 3.00 µm. The reported standard deviation of 29.543 nm describes the spread among accepted fits. All 100 zero-noise trials passed the MSE cutoff even with this offset. Report the offset and the spread separately when judging recovery of the nominal thickness.

At each of the four nonzero noise settings, the archive records no accepted fit. It gives no fitted thickness for those rows. Comparing the two inversion methods would require fitting both to the same spectra under the same noise model.
