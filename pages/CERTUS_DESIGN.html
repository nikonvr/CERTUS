CERTUS-DESIGN

Stochastic Global Synthesis & Density Clustering Engine

Scientific Revision Pro 2026.04.17

Executive Summary: CERTUS-DESIGN is a high-performance synthesis engine for optical thin films, powered by the PGLOBAL (Population-based Stochastic Exploration) algorithm.

Scientific Note (Global Optimum Guarantee): Thin-film optical design in high-dimensional space is an NP-hard problem. While the term "Global" is used historically in PGLOBAL to contrast with purely local descents (like L-BFGS-B), the algorithm performs a robust stochastic exploration combined with topological mutations. It guarantees escape from immediate local minima, but does not provide a mathematical proof of finding the absolute global optimum.
It solves the extreme multimodality of the thin-film design space by combining stochastic exploration with density-based clustering. Key innovations include Analytic Gradient computation (Adjoint Method) for $O(N)$ local refinement, Topological Deep Search (Needle Method) for automatic structure evolution, and incoherent backside correction for real-world glass substrates.

2. The Optimization Landscape

The Merit Function (RMSE) for a multilayer optical coating is non-convex and highly multimodal. For a stack of $N$ layers, the solution space contains approximately $2^N$ local minima. Traditional gradient descent (L-BFGS, Levenberg-Marquardt) is strictly local; it cannot escape the initial "valley." CERTUS-DESIGN employs a global strategy to map the topology of the $N$-dimensional hypercube before committing computational resources to refinement.

Problem Dimension: A 40-layer anti-reflection coating has a 40-dimensional parameter space. With thicknesses ranging from 5 to 500 nm, the volume of this hypercube is $\sim 495^{40} \approx 10^{107}$. Exhaustive search is impossible; intelligent sampling is essential.

3. PGLOBAL: Mathematical Formulation

The core algorithm fits into the class of "Multi-Start with Single-Linkage Clustering" methods, enhanced with stochastic exploration reminiscent of Simulated Annealing (SA) during boundary escapes.

$$ P_{accept}(\Delta E, T) = \exp\left( - \frac{\Delta E}{k \cdot T_n} \right) \quad \text{with} \quad T_n = T_0 \cdot \gamma^n $$

Where $\Delta E$ is the variation in Merit Function (RMSE), $T_0$ is the initial temperature, and $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ is the exponential cooling rate across $n$ iterations. This mechanism prevents the stochastic sampling from getting trapped in shallow local minima.

3.1 Critical Clustering Distance

The algorithm groups sample points into "basins of attraction" to avoid redundant local optimizations. The critical distance threshold $d_c$ is derived from the statistical theory of closest points in a Poisson process:

$$ d_c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}} \left( \Gamma\left(1 + \frac{D}{2}\right) \cdot \alpha \cdot \frac{\ln N}{N} \right)^{1/D} $$
  • $D$: Dimension of the problem (number of layers).
  • $N$: Number of samples in the current population.
  • $\alpha$: Clustering density parameter (typically $0.1 - 0.5$).
  • $\Gamma$: The Gamma function, extending factorial to real numbers.

3.2 Clustering Kernel

A point $\mathbf{x}$ is absorbed into a cluster with seed $\mathbf{x}_{seed}$ if and only if:

Absorption Criteria 1. Proximity: $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{seed}\|_{2} < d_c$
2. Descent: $\text{Cost}(\mathbf{x}_{seed}) < \text{Cost}(\mathbf{x})$

This ensures that we only group points that are geometrically close AND locally descending towards the same minimum. Each undiscovered basin triggers a new L-BFGS-B refinement.

4. Transfer Matrix Method (TMM)

4.1 Characteristic Matrix (Macleod Convention)

CERTUS uses the Macleod convention with complex refractive index $\hat{n} = n - ik$ (k > 0 for absorbing materials) and factor +1j in the off-diagonal elements:

$$ \mathbf{M}_j = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi_j & \frac{+i\sin\phi_j}{\hat{n}_j} \\ +i\hat{n}_j\sin\phi_j & \cos\phi_j \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \phi_j = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\hat{n}_j d_j $$

4.2 System Matrix and Observables

The total system matrix is built by pre-multiplication in Air→Substrate order:

$$ \mathbf{M}_{sys} = \mathbf{M}_{N} \cdot \mathbf{M}_{N-1} \cdots \mathbf{M}_{1} $$

Transmittance for normal incidence ($n_{inc} = 1$, $n_{exit} = n_{sub}$):

$$ T = \frac{4\,\text{Re}(n_{sub})}{|m_{00} + n_{sub}\,m_{01} + m_{10} + n_{sub}\,m_{11}|^2} $$

4.3 Incoherent Backside Correction

For glass substrates (~1 mm), the back surface reflects incoherently. CERTUS uses a dual-matrix approach:

  • Forward matrix $\mathbf{F}$ (pre-multiply Air→Sub): extracts $T_{fwd}$ and $R_{fwd}$.
  • Reverse matrix $\mathbf{R}$ (post-multiply Sub→Air): extracts $R_{back}$.
$$ T_{total} = \frac{T_{fwd} \cdot T_{ext}}{1 - R_{back} \cdot R_{ext}}, \qquad R_{ext} = \left(\frac{n_{sub} - 1}{n_{sub} + 1}\right)^2 $$

5. Analytic Gradient (The Adjoint Method)

To refine the best clusters, CERTUS uses the L-BFGS-B algorithm. This requires the gradient vector $\nabla_{\mathbf{d}} \text{MSE}$. Calculating this numerically (Finite Differences) requires $2N$ matrix evaluations per step ($O(N^2)$). CERTUS implements an Analytic Gradient ($O(N)$) using the Adjoint Matrix method.

5.1 Forward-Backward Propagation

We decompose the total transfer matrix $\mathbf{M}_{total}$ for a stack of $N$ layers. For any layer $j$, we define:

$$ \mathbf{M}_{before}[j] = \prod_{k=0}^{j-1} \mathbf{M}_k \quad \text{(Substrate to Layer } j)$$ $$ \mathbf{M}_{after}[j] = \prod_{k=N}^{j+1} \mathbf{M}_k \quad \text{(Air to Layer } j)$$

Both products are computed in a single $O(N)$ scan and cached.

5.2 Exact Derivative

The derivative of the total matrix with respect to thickness $d_j$ uses the matrix chain rule:

$$ \frac{\partial \mathbf{M}_{total}}{\partial d_j} = \mathbf{M}_{after}[j] \cdot \frac{d\mathbf{M}_j}{d d_j} \cdot \mathbf{M}_{before}[j] $$
$$ \frac{\partial \text{MSE}}{\partial d_j} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=1}^{M} w_k \left(S_{calc}(\lambda_k) - S_{target}(\lambda_k)\right) \frac{\partial S_{calc}}{\partial d_j} $$

Where $\frac{\partial S_{calc}}{\partial d_j}$ is extracted directly from the components of $\frac{\partial \mathbf{M}_{total}}{\partial d_j}$. This explicit Jacobian $\nabla_{\mathbf{d}} \text{MSE}$ is what feeds the L-BFGS-B quasi-Newton step.

The derivative of a single layer matrix $\mathbf{M}_j$ is analytically simple:

$$ \frac{d\mathbf{M}_j}{d d_j} = \frac{2\pi \hat{n}_j}{\lambda} \begin{pmatrix} -\sin\phi_j & \frac{+i\cos\phi_j}{\hat{n}_j} \\ +i\hat{n}_j\cos\phi_j & -\sin\phi_j \end{pmatrix} $$
Performance: This allows calculating the gradients for ALL $N$ layers in a single forward + backward pass, resulting in a 50×–100× speedup compared to numerical differentiation. All gradient kernels are JIT-compiled via Numba in Float64/Complex128.

6. Smart Cleanup & Two-Phase Healing

After global optimization, some layers may converge to negligible thicknesses (< 1 nm). Smart Cleanup removes these and merges adjacent layers of identical materials. This topology change alters the design space, potentially trapping the solution in a suboptimal basin.

6.1 Smart Cleanup

smart_cleanup(update_target=False) 1. Remove all layers with $d < 1.0$ nm (conservative threshold).
2. Merge adjacent layers of the same material: $d_{merged} = d_i + d_{i+1}$.
3. update_target=False: preserve original layer count target
  so the Needle can grow back any removed layers.

6.2 Two-Phase Healing

If cleanup removed layers, the design is healed in two guaranteed phases. The local descent is never skipped, even if the restricted global finds a good solution.

Phase 1 — Restricted Global (mode = 'healing') PGLOBAL search within material-aware bounds:
$$ \Delta d_j = \frac{\lambda_0}{10\, n_j(\lambda_0)} \quad \text{(~40% of QWOT)} $$ Bounds per layer: $[d_j - \Delta d_j,\; d_j + \Delta d_j]$
Config: 1000 × scale samples/iter, 10 iterations, 20 clusters max, 50k evals.
Explores basins in the vicinity of the current solution after topology change.
Phase 2 — Local Polish (mode = 'local') L-BFGS-B within $\pm 2$ nm bounds, using the analytic gradient.
Converges precisely within the best basin found by Phase 1.
This phase is always executed, guaranteeing that the local descent is not missed.
Excursion Example ($\lambda_0 = 550$ nm):
SiO₂ ($n = 1.46$): $\Delta d = 37.7$ nm  |  TiO₂ ($n = 2.30$): $\Delta d = 23.9$ nm  |  Ta₂O₅ ($n = 2.10$): $\Delta d = 26.2$ nm
The excursion is physically motivated and material-dependent, unlike a fixed percentage.

7. Needle Method & Overshoot-Prune Strategy

When the layer count is below the user's target (after cleanup removed layers), the Needle Method (Tikhonravov) is activated to grow the structure. It calculates the topological derivative $P(z)$ across the optical thickness of the stack:

$$ P(z) = \frac{\partial \text{Merit}}{\partial d_{new}} \bigg|_{d_{new} \to 0} $$

If $P(z)$ is strongly negative at position $z$, inserting a zero-thickness layer (mathematical needle) of the alternate material at position $z$ will decrease the merit function.

7.1 Needle Insertion Cycle (3 Steps)

Per-insertion cycle Step 1 (Optim): Insert needle at $z^*$ by splitting the host layer. Run L-BFGS-B on the $(N+2)$-layer stack (left + needle + right).
Step 2 (Cleanup): Smart cleanup — remove thin layers, merge adjacent identical. If layers removed, re-optimize.
Step 3 (Evaluate): Check RMSE improvement vs pre-insertion value. Require > 1% improvement to count as "successful." If stagnation detected 3× consecutively → abort.

7.2 Overshoot & Prune Strategy

On first layer deficit, the system does not grow exactly to the target. Instead, it temporarily overshoots by +20% to enrich the design space, then prunes back:

Overshoot & Prune Algorithm 1. Set temporary target: $N_{overshoot} = \min(\lceil 1.20 \times N_{target} \rceil,\; N_{max})$, with a minimum of $N_{target} + 2$.
2. Grow via iterative Needle insertions until $N_{overshoot}$ is reached.
3. Prune one layer at a time (thinnest first):
  a. Identify thinnest layer: $j = \arg\min_i d_i$.
  b. Remove layer $j$ from the stack.
  c. Merge any newly-adjacent identical materials.
  d. Repeat until $\text{count} \leq N_{target}$.
4. Final L-BFGS-B polish on the pruned stack.
Why one-at-a-time pruning? In alternating H/L stacks, removing one layer makes its two neighbours (same material) adjacent, triggering a merge. This gives a net reduction of 2 layers per prune step. Batch removal would overshoot the pruning target. The +20% overshoot compensates for this effect and provides enough extra layers to explore a richer design space before converging to the final topology.

8. Cost Function & Merit

8.1 Spectral RMSE

$$ \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M} w_j \left(S_{calc}(\lambda_j) - S_{target}(\lambda_j)\right)^2} $$

where $S$ is reflectance or transmittance and $w_j$ are spectral weights (uniform or user-defined).

8.2 Colorimetric Target (Optional)

For decorative coatings, CERTUS supports simultaneous color optimization using the CIE 1976 $\Delta E_{00}$ metric. The perceived color difference between the designed and target coating is computed from the CIELAB coordinates $(L^*, a^*, b^*)$ derived from the CIE 1931 standard observer and D65 illuminant:

$$ \Delta E_{00} = \sqrt{ \left(\frac{\Delta L'}{k_L S_L}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta C'}{k_C S_C}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta H'}{k_H S_H}\right)^2 + R_T \frac{\Delta C'}{k_C S_C} \frac{\Delta H'}{k_H S_H} } $$
