Methodology & Statistical Inversion

Overcoming Photometric Amplitude

A comparison of fringe-position and envelope-amplitude methods, with a historical synthetic result whose run data are not linked here.

The Swanepoel Limit

The Swanepoel envelope method estimates optical constants from measured transmission maxima and minima. Amplitude calibration, scattering and spectral resolution can affect those envelopes.

Transmission amplitude can be affected by roughness, alignment, finite spectral resolution and detector response. These effects need an uncertainty budget before an envelope-derived $n$ or $k$ is interpreted.

If unmodeled transmission loss is attributed entirely to film absorption, an envelope inversion can bias $k$ and $n$. The size and direction of that bias depend on the measurement and model.

Fringe-Position Approach

A position-based approach uses the wavelengths of detected interference extrema. Those positions can provide information about optical thickness, provided the fringes remain resolved and correctly assigned.

Peak locations are not invariant to asymmetric noise, overlapping features or convolution. A position-based method therefore needs a check of wavelength calibration and peak-detection uncertainty.

  • Less direct dependence on absolute intensity calibration
  • Dependence on resolved fringe positions and wavelength calibration
  • Need for an explicit position-error model
